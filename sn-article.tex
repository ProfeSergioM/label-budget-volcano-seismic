\subsection{Volcanic systems and monitoring network}\label{subsec:area}

The study uses the seismic catalog of the Southern Andes Volcanological
Observatory (OVDAS), which monitors the active volcanoes of southern Chile
within the National Geology and Mining Service (SERNAGEOMIN)
\cite{amigo_volcano_2021}. OVDAS runs a local seismic network at each
monitored volcano and keeps a continuous event catalog for each. Five
systems were selected because their catalogs hold large event volumes, cover
together the classes of interest, and come from distinct volcanic
environments, so that the dataset spans contrasting seismicity and recording
conditions.

These environments differ in the processes that drive their seismicity.
Villarrica is an open-conduit system with an active lava lake and
near-continuous long-period (LP) seismicity \cite{richardson_varying_2014}.
Nevados de Chill\'an has erupted since 2016, alternating effusive and
explosive phases \cite{cardona_volcanic_2021}. Copahue hosts persistent
hydrothermal circulation \cite{casas_single-station_2024}. Laguna del Maule
records a prolonged volcano-tectonic (VT) swarm linked to inflation of its
magmatic-hydrothermal system \cite{cardona_crustal_2018}. Hudson is a heavily
glaciated stratovolcano whose catalog combines brittle-fracture,
fluid-resonance and surface-process seismicity
\cite{agurto-detzel_seismicity_2014}. The five systems therefore provide five
distinct seismicity rates and catalog compositions.

The systems span about 1100~km of the Southern Volcanic Zone, from Laguna del
Maule in the north to Hudson in the south (Figure~\ref{fig:map}), and each
contributes the vertical channel of one station, sampled at 100~Hz
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}). The records differ between systems in instrument
response, source--station distance, site conditions and event population,
so every quantity in this paper is computed within one system. The interval
of each system is the span over which its station appears as the detecting
station in the catalog. It covers January 2013 to December 2024 at Nevados
de Chill\'an and Villarrica, and is shorter at the other three: Laguna del
Maule starts in April 2021, after station NI2Z began operating on 30 March
2021; Hudson starts in March 2013, the first month with a continuous record;
and Copahue and Hudson end in August 2024 and January 2024, when their
stations stop appearing as detecting stations.

\subsection{Nature of the catalog}\label{subsec:catalog}

Each catalog entry is the result of an operational classification by an
analyst, who assigns the event class, the detecting station, an onset at
that station and a duration. The onset is the time reference used to
extract every waveform window, and the duration is part of the same entry.
Median durations range from 8.4~s for VT to 36.0~s for TO, with HB, VLP, LP
and AV in between (Table~\ref{tab:durations}), and they also change between
systems for the same class: LP lasts 11.0~s at Copahue and 29.7~s at
Villarrica, and VT 8.0~s at Laguna del Maule and 17.9~s at Villarrica. A
small set of entries, 1{,}457 events or 0.099\% of the analyzed population,
carries a non-positive duration.

\subsection{Event classes}\label{subsec:classes}

The analysis uses six operational classes of the OVDAS database, based on the
classifications of \cite{lahr_earthquake_1994} and \cite{chouet_volcano_2003}:
LP (long-period), VT (volcano-tectonic), HB (hybrid), VLP (very-long-period,
coded LV), AV (avalanche and rockfall) and TO (tornillo). Between 2013 and
2024 these classes account for 63--97\% of the catalog entries of each system
(Table~\ref{tab:rawcounts}). All six are discrete events, which gives the
analysis a common unit of observation; the remaining codes describe
sustained tremor (TR), observational attributes such as a surface
manifestation (EX) or an acoustic signal (MF), and detections below the
classification threshold (VA).

Analysts assign the six classes from waveform morphology and spectral
content (Figure~\ref{fig:gallery}), so they are operational signal categories
and the interpretations below are the ones commonly attached to each label.
LP events show emergent onsets and narrow-band low-frequency energy, usually
attributed to resonance in fluid-filled cracks \cite{chouet_long-period_1996}.
VT events show impulsive onsets and broadband energy extending to higher
frequencies, consistent with brittle rock failure. HB events combine an
impulsive onset with a low-frequency coda \cite{white_volcano-tectonic_2016}.
VLP events carry energy at lower frequencies than LP and are linked to slow
fluid transport or conduit deformation \cite{almendros_identifying_2002}. AV
events are emergent, broadband and long surface signals
\cite{heck_localization_2019}, and TO events show slowly decaying,
quasi-monochromatic codas \cite{gomez_m_tornillo_1999}. The classes overlap in
waveform and spectral features, and HB in particular lies between LP and VT.

\subsection{Class composition}\label{subsec:composition}

The analyzed population is the part of the six-class catalog detected at
the station of Table~\ref{tab:stations} with a complete record around the
event. The station condition removes most entries at every system except
Villarrica, whose catalog is already dominated by its station, and the two
conditions together retain 73--95\% of the catalog (Table~\ref{tab:filters}).
The catalog also repeats 1{,}000 entries with the same identifier and time,
928 at Nevados de Chill\'an, 48 at Villarrica and 24 at Copahue; each is
counted once, which leaves 1{,}477{,}410 distinct events
(Table~\ref{tab:counts}).

Class composition varies strongly between systems. LP makes up 99.3\% of the
population at Villarrica and 87.2\% at Nevados de Chill\'an, VT 99.5\% at
Laguna del Maule, and HB 58.6\% at Copahue. Hudson has the most even
composition, with 44.0\% VT, 32.0\% LP, 22.5\% AV and 1.4\% HB. The minority
classes follow the same uneven pattern: HB is substantial only at Copahue and
Hudson, TO appears only at Nevados de Chill\'an, VLP concentrates at Nevados
de Chill\'an and Copahue, and AV reaches its largest share at Hudson, a
heavily glaciated edifice, followed by Nevados de Chill\'an with 6.1\%. Two
cells hold a single event, one HB at Villarrica and one AV at Copahue. These
proportions reflect the seismicity of each volcano together with network
sensitivity, station placement and labeling practice.

\subsection{Validation catalogs}\label{subsec:validation}

Four catalogs compiled by other agencies validate the descriptors and the
classifiers on records the OVDAS systems do not contain
(Table~\ref{tab:validation}). They come from other volcanoes, other networks
and other labeling practices, so they test whether what the descriptors
capture at the OVDAS systems holds when the volcano, the station and the
analyst change. This validation is the second stage of the work started in
\cite{morales_recurrence_2026}, which defined the recurrence descriptors
and classified LP, VT and HB over the OVDAS catalog with all its volcanoes
pooled. The study uses every class each
catalog labels, in two groups. LP and VT, present in all four catalogs and at
every OVDAS system, are the global labels. The classes particular to one
catalog or one observatory are the local labels: HB at Nevado del Ruiz,
tremor, explosions and noise in Alaska and Mexico, regional and other events
in Alaska, and noise and tremor at Deception Island, alongside HB, TO, VLP and
AV at the OVDAS systems.

The Deception Island dataset \cite{cortes_standardization_2019}, deposited in
\cite{cortes_vsr_2018}, holds two deployments on this Antarctic volcano,
recorded at 50~Hz: a short-period sensor between the Argentinean and Spanish
bases in 1995, and a broadband station near the Spanish base, about 900~m
away, in 2009. Its label file gives the start and end of every event, and a
single record often holds several labeled events, as the volcano produces
swarms. The study uses every event of both deployments: LP, VT and NTR, the
catalog's class for noise and tremor.

VCSEIS \cite{zhong_deep-learning-based_2024}, distributed in SeisBench format
\cite{woollam_seisbench_2022}, covers four regions catalogued by the Alaska
Volcano Observatory, the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, the Northern
California Earthquake Data Center and the Pacific Northwest Seismic Network
(Cascade Range). Each trace is sampled at 100~Hz and carries P and S
arrivals, and its class comes from the catalog field that separates
long-period from local events. The catalog stores one trace per station, so
an earthquake recorded at several stations appears several times. The study
uses a random draw of 500 traces per region and class. The Alaska catalog
also labels regional earthquakes, tremor, avalanches and events of other
types, and the Hawaiian catalog one explosion and one regional earthquake;
the study draws up to 500 traces of each of these classes.

The VOISS-Net training set \cite{fee_generalized_2025} labels 120~s windows
of continuous data in Alaska and Hawai`i, one label per window and network.
Its Earthquake and Long Period labels correspond to VT and LP, and each
volcano contributes one of them: VT at K\={\i}lauea, Redoubt and Tanaga, LP at
Veniaminof, Semisopochnoi and Pavlof. Its other labels enter as local
classes: harmonic, monochromatic and broadband tremor at Pavlof,
Semisopochnoi and Veniaminof, and harmonic tremor at Popocat\'epetl; and
explosions and noise at Pavlof and Semisopochnoi. The waveforms come from the EarthScope
data center, at the vertical channel of one station per volcano, with 60~s
before and 120~s after each labeled window.

The Servicio Geol\'ogico Colombiano (SGC) provided 500 events per class from
Nevado del Ruiz, labeled LP, VT, HB and tremor by its analysts. Each event comes as
one file holding the whole network, sampled at 100~Hz, without catalog onset
or duration. The SGC designates Olleta as the reference station of the
volcano and Bis as its backup.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methods}

The pipeline turns each event into sixteen descriptors computed on two
windows of its waveform: a fixed-length window that opens at the onset, and
a window that follows the event to the end of its coda. The steps appear in
the order in which they are applied, from the station to the descriptors, their
transferability and the transfer and grouping designs, and each parameter is given with the step that uses
it.

\subsection{Station selection}\label{subsec:stationselection}

Each OVDAS system uses one station, the one that combines the largest number
of catalogued detections with uninterrupted operation
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}). One station per system keeps a single instrument
response and source--station geometry within each class. The validation
catalogs follow the same rule, with each region, volcano or station treated
as a system of its own.

\subsection{Fixed-length window}\label{subsec:conditioning}

The first window spans 10~s, 1{,}000 samples at 100~Hz, from the
analyst-assigned onset, so every sample belongs to the event. It is cut from
a padded segment from 7~s before the onset to 13~s after it, which is
linearly detrended and band-pass filtered between 0.5 and 10~Hz with a
zero-phase fourth-order Butterworth filter; the 3~s margins at both ends
absorb the edge transient of the filter. The amplitude response of the
filter is 50.0\% at 0.5~Hz, 95.7\% at 0.7~Hz and 99.9\% from 1.0~Hz, and
98.6\% at 6.5~Hz, 89.4\% at 8~Hz and 50.0\% at 10~Hz. The padded segment
comes from one gap-free trace of the selected station, so an event with a
gap, an interruption or a date before the station was installed falls
outside the analyzed population, which is the second condition of
Table~\ref{tab:filters}. An event whose filtered window has zero variance is
excluded as well, since its z-score is undefined.

The signal-to-noise ratio of each event is measured on the same padded
segment, between a noise window of 2~s, from 4 to 2~s before the onset, and
the signal window:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNR_{dB}} = 20\log_{10}\frac{\mathrm{RMS}(\text{signal window})}{\mathrm{RMS}(\text{noise window})}.
\end{equation}
The 2~s gap keeps an emergent arrival out of the noise window, and the band
of 0.5 to 10~Hz keeps out the secondary oceanic microseism, which dominates
the record at lower frequencies. The ratio therefore measures recording
quality where the events radiate, with VLP, whose energy extends below the
band, as the one class it understates. It separates a class that stands out
because of where it is recorded from one that stands out because of its
source. Before any descriptor is computed, the signal window is z-scored to
zero mean and unit variance (Figure~\ref{fig:window}).

\subsection{Coda-length window}\label{subsec:codawindow}

The fixed-length window covers the whole event for 11.9\% of the analyzed
population: 71.8\% at Copahue, 67.5\% at Laguna del Maule, 34.6\% at Hudson,
4.4\% at Nevados de Chill\'an and 0.2\% at Villarrica. For the other events it
measures the onset and the early coda. A second window therefore follows each
event to the end of its coda, so that the two windows together show which
properties of a class lie in its onset and which in its full coda. The
sixteen descriptors are computed on both windows for the same events.

The coda-length window opens at the onset and spans the duration of the
event, from 5~s, the shortest record that supports the recurrence
descriptors, to 100~s, 10{,}000 samples, after which a long coda is measured
on its first 100~s. At the OVDAS systems the duration is the analyst-assigned
one, and events shorter than 5~s, including those with non-positive
duration, keep only the fixed-length window. The window is cut from a padded
segment from 10~s before the onset to 3~s after the end of the coda and is
conditioned with the same detrending and band-pass as the fixed-length
window, so the two windows differ only in length. The shared band matters
most here, since below 0.5~Hz the oceanic microseism would weigh on the
recurrence descriptors of a long window as much as the event itself at
coastal and island stations. The noise window spans 5~s, from 7 to 2~s before
the onset, and the signal-to-noise ratio uses the same band as before
(Figure~\ref{fig:codawindow}).

\subsection{Manual segmentation of the validation records}\label{subsec:manualcut}

The validation catalogs give labels but not the three references the windows
need: an onset, the end of the coda and a pre-event noise window. One analyst
therefore sets the three by hand for every validation event, with a single
protocol for the four catalogs (Figure~\ref{fig:manualcut}).

Each record is shown as its trace, band-passed between 0.5 and 10~Hz, and
its spectrogram up to 25~Hz, together with the reference the catalog
supplies: the labeled segment at Deception Island, the P and S arrivals in
VCSEIS, or the 120~s labeled window in VOISS-Net. The display starts from an
automatic proposal that the analyst accepts or corrects. The proposed onset
is the catalog arrival or segment start where one exists, and otherwise the
first trigger, within the labeled window, of a recursive STA/LTA detector
(0.5 and 5~s windows, thresholds of 3 and 1, record band-passed between 1 and
10~Hz). The proposed end of the coda is the catalog segment end where one
exists, and otherwise the point where a smoothed envelope of the record
falls below 1.2 times its pre-event level for 2~s. The proposed noise window
spans 12 to 2~s before the onset. The analyst then places the onset at the
first arrival of the labeled event, the end of the coda where the signal
returns to the pre-event level, and the noise window right before the onset,
clear of other events where the record allows it.

An event is kept when its onset falls within the catalog reference and its
coda ends within the record, and it is discarded when no event is visible in
that reference or when it lasts less than 5~s. It enters each window whose
padded segment its record holds. At Nevado del Ruiz, events are reviewed at
Olleta, and those not visible there are reviewed at Bis, as the SGC
indicates; the station of each event is recorded, and Olleta and Bis form two
systems, since the station changes the record of a class. VOISS-Net windows
are reviewed in random order within each volcano and class. The analyst may
also revise a label; an event relabeled as a tornillo (TO) stays in LP, its
catalogued class, with a flag.

Each event is kept once. Copies of the same earthquake at several VCSEIS
stations share a catalog source identifier, and an event spanning two
consecutive VOISS-Net windows or SGC files gives onsets less than 3~s apart;
of each group, the copy with its onset inside the labeled reference and the
highest signal-to-noise ratio is kept. Records sampled at 50 or 40~Hz are
resampled to 100~Hz by polyphase filtering, with factors of 2 and 5/2, before
any filtering. From there, both windows and the sixteen descriptors are
computed with the same code and parameters as at the OVDAS systems, using
the manual onset and end of coda.

Explosions and the VCSEIS classes outside LP and VT follow the same protocol,
since they carry a catalog arrival or a distinct first motion. For the
regional and other events of Alaska, whose proposal starts at the catalog P
arrival, the analyst reviews a sample of each class and accepts the proposal
for the rest. Tremor, noise
and the Deception Island NTR class are continuous signals with no onset, and
they enter through the fixed-length window alone, centered on the labeled
span: the 120~s window in VOISS-Net, the whole record at Nevado del Ruiz and
the labeled segment at Deception Island. Their noise window falls within the
signal itself, so no signal-to-noise ratio is assigned to them.

\subsection{Class support criterion}\label{subsec:classsupport}

A class enters the per-class analyses of a system when it holds at least 50
events there. At that size a class represents the range it spans at the
system, and its measured properties describe the class rather than a few
events, while local classes recorded at a single volcano, such as
monochromatic tremor at Semisopochnoi, still enter. The threshold is set in advance and applied to every volcano-class
combination, so the classes of each system do not depend on any later
result; a combination below it stays in the analyzed population. Eighteen
of the thirty OVDAS combinations clear it (Table~\ref{tab:counts}): five at
Nevados de Chill\'an, four at Copahue and Hudson, three at Villarrica and two
at Laguna del Maule. Every system thus contributes at least two classes, and
no class reaches the threshold at all five. The same threshold applies to
every validation system.

\subsection{RQA and spectral feature extraction}\label{subsec:features}

Each window yields sixteen descriptors, thirteen from recurrence
quantification analysis (RQA) and three from the spectrum, all computed on
the signal window of one event with the same parameters for both windows.

The recurrence descriptors $DET$, $L$, $L_{max}$, $DIV$, $L_{entr}$, $LAM$,
$TT$, $V_{max}$, $V_{entr}$, $W$, $W_{max}$, $W_{div}$ and $W_{entr}$
\cite{marwan_recurrence_2007} are computed on the z-scored window with an
embedding dimension of 2, a delay of 3 samples and a Theiler correction of 1,
and diagonal, vertical and white vertical lines are counted from a minimum
length of 2 samples (Figure~\ref{fig:rqa}). The neighborhood radius
$\varepsilon$ is set for each event from the grid $\{0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$: the
first radius with a recurrence rate ($RR$) within $[0.01, 0.10]$ is used,
and otherwise the one with $RR$ closest to the midpoint of $[0.01, 0.05]$.
$DIV$ and $W_{div}$, reciprocals of a longest-line length, are left missing
when that length is zero, and all thirteen descriptors are left missing when
no radius gives a usable recurrence plot.

The spectral descriptors come from the FFT of the filtered window before
z-scoring, over 1.0 to 8.0~Hz, the flat part of the filter response.
$f_{\text{peak}}$ is the frequency of maximum amplitude in that band, and
$SR$ is the ratio of mean to maximum amplitude \cite{cui_subdivision_2021},
low for energy concentrated in a narrow band and close to 1 for a flat
spectrum. $FI$ compares two bands separated by a gap
\cite{cui_subdivision_2021,buurman_seismic_2010}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FI} = \log_{10}\frac{\overline{A}(4.5\text{--}8.0~\mathrm{Hz})}{\overline{A}(1.0\text{--}2.5~\mathrm{Hz})},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{A}$ is the mean spectral amplitude of each band. One band
applies to every event, which keeps the three descriptors comparable. VLP
events radiate below this band, so for them $f_{\text{peak}}$ gives the
strongest component the band admits, a lower bound on their dominant
frequency; for the other five classes it measures the dominant frequency
itself (Figure~\ref{fig:spectral}).

\subsection{Descriptor transferability}\label{subsec:transferability}

A descriptor is transferable when it separates the global classes in the same
direction and with a similar strength at every system, whatever the volcano,
the station or the analyst. Each of the sixteen descriptors is therefore
scored at each system holding LP and VT above the support criterion by the
area under the ROC curve (AUC) of LP against VT, a measure that needs no
classifier and no threshold. The AUC is oriented by its median sign across
systems, so a value below 0.5 at one system marks a reversal of the
separation there. Descriptors are ranked by the median of the oriented AUC
across systems, penalized by its interquartile range: a high median with a
small spread identifies a descriptor that separates LP from VT everywhere,
and a high median with a large spread one that separates them only at some
systems. The ranking is computed on each window, and its two leading
descriptors define the plane in which the global and local classes of all
systems are compared.

\subsection{Class heterogeneity}\label{subsec:heterogeneity}

A label groups the events an analyst judges alike, and the same label may
gather signals of more than one kind. The number of groups within each class
of each system is measured with a Gaussian mixture model on the sixteen
descriptors, scaled with the median and interquartile range of the class and
reduced by principal components to 90\% of their variance. The number of
components, from 1 to 6, is selected by the Bayesian information criterion.
Since that criterion favors more components as the sample grows, every class
is evaluated on draws of 100 events, repeated 20 times, and LP and VT of each
system serve as the reference. For the classes that split, the mixture is fit
on all their events, and each component is described by its size, its median
descriptors, its duration and its signal-to-noise ratio. Every event keeps its
label, so the components describe the content of a class without changing
the population that enters the following steps.

\subsection{Classification and transfer design}\label{subsec:transfer}

Generalization is measured by training a classifier on one system and
scoring it on another. Each model is trained on a single source system, so
each score describes the passage from one recorded population to another,
and the scores of all source--target pairs form a matrix for each window.
Two matrices are built, one centered on the hybrid class and one covering
every system.

The first matrix holds LP, VT and HB, the classes that Nevado del Ruiz shares
with the OVDAS taxonomy. HB lies between LP and VT, with an impulsive onset
and a low-frequency coda, and its boundary depends most on the analyst who
draws it. Its sources are the systems with the three classes above the
support criterion, Copahue, Hudson and Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, and Nevado
del Ruiz at Bis enters as a target scored on its LP and HB events. The pairs
of this matrix change the station within one volcano, from Olleta to Bis; the
volcano within one observatory, between Copahue and Hudson; and both,
between Nevado del Ruiz and the OVDAS systems, with HB labeled by the
analysts of two agencies.

The second matrix holds LP and VT across every system. Its sources are the
systems with both classes above the support criterion, the five OVDAS
systems, the four VCSEIS regions and Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, and every
system is a target, including those with a single class: the six VOISS-Net
volcanoes, Nevado del Ruiz at Bis and the two Deception Island deployments.
Its pairs extend the same sequence, from one volcano recorded by two
deployments at Deception Island, to volcanoes of one observatory, to
volcanoes of different observatories. In both matrices the diagonal, a
cross-validation within each system, is the reference, and the difference
between a transfer score and the diagonal score of its target is the
transfer loss.

Two classifiers are trained on each source. Gradient-boosted decision trees
\cite{chen_xgboost_2016} use the sixteen descriptors at their native scales,
since a tree ensemble is invariant to monotone rescaling. An
$L_2$-regularized logistic regression uses the descriptors standardized with
the mean and variance of the source. The two models differ in flexibility,
so a transfer loss shared by both comes from the descriptors and the
records, and one seen only in the boosted trees comes from what that model
learned from its source.

Each training set holds 100 events per class drawn at random from the
source, a size that every class of every source exceeds in both windows. A common
size keeps sources of very different volume comparable, and an equal count
per class keeps the class prior of the source out of the decision. The draw
is repeated 20 times with different seeds, and each score is reported as the
mean and spread of the repetitions.

Hyperparameters are tuned on the source alone. For the boosted trees, a
tree-structured Parzen estimator \cite{akiba_optuna_2019} runs 200 trials
scored by stratified five-fold cross-validation on an independent draw of the
source, over boosting rounds in $[200, 2000]$, maximum depth in $[3, 12]$,
learning rate in $[0.01, 0.3]$, row and column subsampling in $[0.5, 1.0]$,
minimum child weight in $[1, 50]$, and $L_1$ and $L_2$ regularization in
$[10^{-8}, 10]$, with the learning rate, child weight and both regularization
terms sampled log-uniformly. For the logistic regression, the same
cross-validation selects the regularization strength from a logarithmic grid
between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{3}$. The selected configuration of a source stays
fixed across its 20 draws and all its targets.

Each target is scored by the recall of every class it holds and, when it
holds all the classes of the matrix, by their geometric mean,
\begin{equation}
G_m = \left( \prod_{c=1}^{C} \mathrm{recall}_c \right)^{1/C},
\end{equation}
which rewards recovering every class regardless of their proportion in the
target. Scores are computed on all the events of the target and reported
with the distribution of their signal-to-noise ratio, which separates a loss
that follows recording quality from one that follows the source. The design
runs on both windows, and the two sets of matrices show which window carries
each class from one system to another. The permutation importance of each
descriptor, computed on every source--target pair, shows which descriptors
the transferred models rely on, and is compared with the ranking of
Section~\ref{subsec:transferability}.

\subsection{Unsupervised grouping}\label{subsec:unsupervised}

The last step removes the labels from training altogether. The events of each
system, global and local classes together, are grouped with a Gaussian
mixture model on the transferable descriptors, each standardized with the
median and interquartile range of its system, which requires no label. The
number of components is selected by the Bayesian information criterion
between 2 and 10. The grouping runs within each system and on all systems
together, and its agreement with the catalog labels, applied only after the
grouping, is measured by the adjusted Rand index, the normalized mutual
information and the share of each class that falls in its majority group. A
class that forms its own group at systems where no model was trained shows
that the descriptors carry it without supervision, and a local class that
falls in a group apart from LP and VT shows that the same descriptors
distinguish the classes particular to one observatory.

The grouping of all systems together also tests for classes beyond the
labels. A component that recurs at several systems and catalogs, holds few
LP and VT events, and gathers events that each center labels in its own way
is a candidate for a further global class, and it is described by the labels
it collects at each system and by its median descriptors.
