\subsection{Volcanic systems and monitoring network}\label{subsec:area}

The study uses the seismic catalog of the Southern Andes Volcanological
Observatory (OVDAS), which monitors the active volcanoes of southern Chile
within the National Geology and Mining Service (SERNAGEOMIN)
\cite{amigo_volcano_2021} and keeps a continuous event catalog for the local
seismic network of each volcano. Five systems were selected for their large
event volumes, their joint coverage of the classes of interest and their
distinct volcanic environments.

These environments differ in the processes that drive their seismicity.
Villarrica is an open-conduit system with an active lava lake and
near-continuous long-period (LP) seismicity \cite{richardson_varying_2014}.
Nevados de Chill\'an has erupted since 2016, alternating effusive and
explosive phases \cite{cardona_volcanic_2021}. Copahue hosts persistent
hydrothermal circulation \cite{casas_single-station_2024}. Laguna del Maule
records a prolonged volcano-tectonic (VT) swarm linked to inflation of its
magmatic-hydrothermal system \cite{cardona_crustal_2018}. Hudson is a heavily
glaciated stratovolcano whose catalog combines brittle-fracture,
fluid-resonance and surface-process seismicity
\cite{agurto-detzel_seismicity_2014}.

The systems span about 1100~km of the Southern Volcanic Zone
(Figure~\ref{fig:map}), and each contributes the vertical channel of one
station, sampled at 100~Hz, over the interval covered by its analyzed events
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}). The records differ between systems in instrument
response, source--station distance, site conditions and event population.
Laguna del Maule starts in April 2021, after station NI2Z began operating on
30 March 2021. Copahue holds 99.1\% of its analyzed events between 2013 and
2016, and its station detects 979 events from 2017 to its last event in
August 2024. Hudson has 28 events in March 2013 and a record from June 2013
to January 2024 that includes months without events.

\subsection{Nature of the catalog}\label{subsec:catalog}

assigns the event class, the detecting station, an onset at that station and

non-positive duration appears in 654 entries at Nevados de Chill\'an, 514 at
Villarrica, 267 at Copahue, 22 at Hudson and none at Laguna del Maule, at most
0.24\% of the analyzed events of a system.

\subsection{Event classes}\label{subsec:classes}

The analysis uses six operational classes of the OVDAS database, based on the
classifications of \cite{lahr_earthquake_1994} and \cite{chouet_volcano_2003}:
LP (long-period), VT (volcano-tectonic), HB (hybrid), VLP (very-long-period,
coded LV), AV (avalanche and rockfall) and TO (tornillo), which account for
63--97\% of the catalog entries of each system between 2013 and 2024
(Table~\ref{tab:rawcounts}). All six are discrete events, which gives the
analysis a common unit of observation; the remaining codes describe sustained
tremor (TR), observational attributes, detections below the classification
threshold and further operational codes.

Analysts assign the six classes from waveform morphology and spectral
content (Figure~\ref{fig:gallery}), so they are operational signal
categories, and the interpretations below are those commonly attached to
each label. LP events show emergent onsets and narrow-band low-frequency
energy, usually attributed to resonance in fluid-filled cracks
\cite{chouet_long-period_1996}. VT events show impulsive onsets and broadband
energy extending to higher frequencies, consistent with brittle rock failure.
HB events combine an impulsive onset with a low-frequency coda
\cite{white_volcano-tectonic_2016}. VLP events carry energy at lower
frequencies than LP and are linked to slow fluid transport or conduit
deformation \cite{almendros_identifying_2002}. AV events are emergent,
broadband and long surface signals \cite{heck_localization_2019}, and TO
events show slowly decaying, quasi-monochromatic codas
\cite{gomez_m_tornillo_1999}. The classes overlap in waveform and spectral
features, and HB in particular lies between LP and VT.

Class composition varies strongly between systems. LP makes up 99.3\% of the
population at Villarrica and 87.2\% at Nevados de Chill\'an, VT 99.5\% at

\subsection{Validation catalogs}\label{subsec:validation}

Four catalogs compiled by other agencies provide labeled records from other
volcanoes, networks and labeling practices (Table~\ref{tab:validation}), and
the study uses every class each catalog labels. LP and VT, present in all
four catalogs and at every OVDAS system, are the global labels; the classes
particular to one catalog or one observatory, HB, TO, VLP and AV at the OVDAS
systems and the other classes of Table~\ref{tab:validation}, are the local
labels. The events analyzed at each validation system are given in
Table~\ref{tab:valcounts} (Appendix~\ref{secA1}).

The Deception Island dataset
\cite{cortes_standardization_2019,cortes_vsr_2018} holds two deployments on this Antarctic volcano,
recorded at 50~Hz: a short-period sensor between the Argentinean and Spanish
bases in 1995 and a broadband station near the Spanish base in 2009. Its
label file gives the start and end of every event, often several per record,
as the volcano produces swarms. The study uses every LP, VT and NTR event,
the catalog's class for noise and tremor signals, and each deployment forms a
system of its own.

VCSEIS \cite{zhong_deep-learning-based_2024}, distributed in SeisBench format
\cite{woollam_seisbench_2022}, covers four regions catalogued by the Alaska
Volcano Observatory, the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, the Northern
California Earthquake Data Center and the Pacific Northwest Seismic Network
(Cascade Range). Each trace is sampled at 100~Hz, carries P and S arrivals and
takes its class from the catalog field that separates long-period from local
events; an earthquake recorded at several stations appears once per station.
The study draws at random 500 LP and 500 VT traces per region and up to 500
traces of each further Alaska class: regional earthquakes, tremor, avalanches
and other events.

The VOISS-Net training set \cite{fee_generalized_2025} labels 120~s windows
of continuous data at volcanoes in Alaska, Hawai`i and Mexico, one label per
window and network. Its Earthquake and Long Period labels correspond to VT
and LP, and each volcano contributes one of them: VT at K\={\i}lauea,
Redoubt and Tanaga, LP at Veniaminof, Semisopochnoi and Pavlof. Its other
labels enter as local classes: harmonic, monochromatic and broadband tremor
at Pavlof and Semisopochnoi, harmonic tremor at Veniaminof and at
Popocat\'epetl, its only class, and explosions and noise at Pavlof and
Semisopochnoi. The study takes every labeled VT and LP window and up to 500
windows per volcano of each local class, drawn at random. The waveforms come
from the EarthScope data center, at the vertical channel of a reference
station per volcano or of the next station of a fixed list when it lacks the
window, from 60~s before to 120~s after each labeled window.

The Servicio Geol\'ogico Colombiano (SGC) provided 500 records per class,
labeled LP, VT, HB and tremor by its analysts; the letter R of the file
extension marks those from Nevado del Ruiz: 498 LP, 394 VT, 500 HB and 500
tremor, the last split into 250 of continuous tremor and 250 of tremor
pulses. Each record comes as one file with the stations available at the
time, sampled at a nominal 100~Hz, without catalog onset or duration. The SGC
designates Olleta as the reference station of the volcano and Bis as its
backup. The continuous-tremor files do not include Olleta, so continuous
tremor is recorded at Bis and tremor pulses at Olleta. At Olleta, 201 HB and
2 LP events come from OLLE, the single-component sensor of the older network,
taken as vertical, and the others from the vertical channel OLLZ.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methods}

The pipeline turns each event into sixteen descriptors computed on a
fixed-length window of its waveform and asks how many expert labels a new
dataset needs. A classifier trained on one dataset, the source, is applied to
another, the target, first with no target labels and then with a growing
budget of them; the same comparison is repeated with the onset and end of the
validation events set automatically, and the expert labels themselves are
audited within each dataset and across datasets. Every score is measured
within one system or one pair of systems, and every summary across systems
is built from those values.

\subsection{Station selection}\label{subsec:stationselection}

Each OVDAS system uses one station, the one that combines the largest number
of catalogued detections with uninterrupted operation
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}), which keeps a single instrument response and
source--station geometry within each class. Each validation catalog is
divided into systems following its own recording (Table~\ref{tab:recording}):
a VOISS-Net volcano gathers the windows of its reference station and of the
stations that replace it, a VCSEIS region the traces of all its stations, and
each SGC station and each Deception Island deployment forms a system of its
own.

\subsection{Signal window}\label{subsec:conditioning}

The window spans 10~s, 1{,}000 samples at 100~Hz, from the
analyst-assigned onset. It is cut from a segment padded from 7~s before the
onset to 13~s after it, linearly detrended and band-pass filtered between 0.5
and 10~Hz with a zero-phase fourth-order Butterworth filter; the 3~s margins
absorb the edge transient of the filter. The padded segment comes from one
gap-free trace of the selected station, so an event with a gap, an
interruption or a date before the station was installed falls outside the
analyzed population, the second condition of Table~\ref{tab:filters}, as
does an event whose filtered window has zero variance.

The signal-to-noise ratio of each event compares the signal window with a
2~s noise window, from 4 to 2~s before the onset, on the same filtered
segment:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNR_{dB}} = 20\log_{10}\frac{\mathrm{RMS}(\text{signal window})}{\mathrm{RMS}(\text{noise window})}.
\end{equation}
The 2~s gap keeps an emergent arrival out of the noise window, and the band
of 0.5 to 10~Hz keeps out the secondary oceanic microseism, so the ratio
measures recording quality where the events radiate and, for VLP, the part of
the event within the band. The signal window is then z-scored to zero mean
and unit variance (Figure~\ref{fig:window}). A second window that follows
each event to the end of its coda serves as a sensitivity check of the
transfer matrices (Appendix~\ref{secA1}).

\subsection{Manual segmentation of the validation records}\label{subsec:manualcut}

The validation catalogs give labels but not the onset and end of the coda
that the windows need. One analyst therefore sets or confirms both for every
validation event, together with a pre-event noise window, under a single
protocol for the four catalogs (Figure~\ref{fig:manualcut}).

Each record is shown as its trace, band-passed between 0.5 and 10~Hz, and its
spectrogram up to 25~Hz, with the catalog reference and an automatic proposal that the analyst
accepts or corrects. The proposed onset is the catalog arrival or segment
start where one exists, and otherwise the first trigger of a recursive
STA/LTA detector (0.5 and 5~s windows, thresholds of 3 and 1, record
band-passed between 1 and 10~Hz) at least 5.5~s into the record, within the
labeled window or, for the SGC files, anywhere in the record. The proposed
end of the coda is the catalog segment end where one exists, and otherwise
the point where the envelope, smoothed over 1~s, falls below 1.2 times its
pre-event median for 2~s, with a proposed duration of at least 5~s; the
proposed noise window spans 12 to 2~s before the onset. The analyst places
the onset at the first arrival of the labeled event, the end of the coda
where the signal returns to the pre-event level, and the noise window right
before the onset, clear of other events where the record allows it. The
signal-to-noise ratio is measured as at the OVDAS systems,
from the onset set by the analyst; the manual noise window serves to choose
among copies of one event.

An event is kept when the analyst identifies the labeled event in the record
and its coda ends within the record, and it is discarded when no event is
visible in the catalog reference or when it lasts less than 5~s. The onset of
a kept event falls within the reference, from 2~s before its start, or, with
a flag, just outside it. Events marked as uncertain are kept, and an event
enters each window whose padded segment its record holds. At Nevado del Ruiz,
events are reviewed at Olleta and, when not visible there, at Bis, as the SGC
indicates, so Olleta and Bis differ both in the station and in the events
each records. VOISS-Net windows are reviewed in random order within each
volcano and class. An event the analyst relabels as a tornillo (TO) is
analyzed as LP, with a flag.

Each event is kept once. Of the copies of one earthquake at several VCSEIS
stations, which share a catalog source identifier, or of an event spanning
two consecutive VOISS-Net windows or SGC files, whose onsets lie less than
3~s apart, the copy with its onset inside the reference and the highest ratio
of the event to the manual noise window is kept. Records sampled at 50 or
40~Hz are resampled to 100~Hz by polyphase filtering before any other
filtering, and the window and the sixteen descriptors are then computed
with the same code and parameters as at the OVDAS systems.

Explosions and the further VCSEIS classes, regional, tremor, avalanche and
other events, follow the same protocol, since they carry a catalog arrival or
a distinct first motion; of the regional and other events of Alaska, the
analyst reviews 26 and 228, respectively, and accepts the proposal, the catalog P arrival,
for the rest. The tremor of VOISS-Net and Nevado del Ruiz, noise and the
Deception Island NTR class are continuous signals with no onset, and their
window is centered on the labeled span:
the 120~s window in VOISS-Net, the whole record at Nevado del Ruiz and the
labeled segment at Deception Island, extended where it is shorter than
10~s. Their window thus holds sustained signal rather than a first arrival, and
they carry no signal-to-noise ratio, since their noise window would fall
within the signal.

\subsection{Class support criterion}\label{subsec:classsupport}

A class enters the per-class analyses of a system when it holds at least 50
events there, a threshold that admits the local classes recorded at a single
volcano alongside the global ones. It is fixed before any analysis and
applies to every volcano-class combination of the OVDAS and validation
systems; a combination below it stays in the analyzed population.

\subsection{RQA and spectral feature extraction}\label{subsec:features}

Each window yields sixteen descriptors, thirteen from recurrence
quantification analysis (RQA) and three from the spectrum, computed on the
signal window of one event.

The recurrence descriptors $DET$, $L$, $L_{max}$, $DIV$, $L_{entr}$, $LAM$,
$TT$, $V_{max}$, $V_{entr}$, $W$, $W_{max}$, $W_{div}$ and $W_{entr}$
\cite{marwan_recurrence_2007} are those defined by
\cite{morales_recurrence_2026}, computed on the z-scored window with the
embedding that work selected on the OVDAS catalog by classifier performance:
dimension 2, delay of 3 samples and Theiler correction of 1, with diagonal,
vertical and white vertical lines counted from a length of 2 samples
(Figure~\ref{fig:rqa}). The radius $\varepsilon$ is set for each event from
the grid $\{0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$ as the first with a recurrence rate ($RR$)
between 0.01 and 0.10, or otherwise the one with $RR$ closest to 0.03. $DIV$
and $W_{div}$ are the reciprocals of $L_{max}$ and $W_{max}$, so each pair
orders the events identically; they are left missing when that length is
zero, and all thirteen descriptors are left missing when every radius fails.
Where a later step needs complete values, a missing descriptor takes the
median of its system where each system is scaled on its own, and otherwise
the median of the training data for the logistic regression, while the
boosted trees take it as missing.

Three spectral descriptors complement this set. They come from the FFT of the
filtered window before z-scoring, over 1.0 to 8.0~Hz, where the filter passes
at least 89\% of the amplitude. $f_{\text{peak}}$ is the frequency of maximum
amplitude in that band, and $SR$ the ratio of mean to maximum amplitude
\cite{cui_subdivision_2021}, low for energy concentrated in a narrow band and
close to 1 for a flat spectrum. $FI$ compares two bands separated by a gap
\cite{cui_subdivision_2021,buurman_seismic_2010}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FI} = \log_{10}\frac{\overline{A}(4.5\text{--}8.0~\mathrm{Hz})}{\overline{A}(1.0\text{--}2.5~\mathrm{Hz})},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{A}$ is the mean spectral amplitude of each band. One band
applies to every event, which keeps the descriptors comparable, so
$f_{\text{peak}}$ measures the dominant frequency of the classes that radiate
within it, LP and TO and most HB and AV events, and bounds it for the others:
it lies above the dominant frequency of VLP, which radiates mostly below
1~Hz, and at or below that of VT, which often exceeds 8~Hz
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectral}).

A descriptor is transferable when it separates the global classes in the same
direction and with a similar strength at every system. Each descriptor is
scored at each system holding LP and VT above the support criterion by the
area under the ROC curve (AUC) of LP against VT, which needs no classifier
and no threshold, oriented by its median direction across systems, so a value
below 0.5 marks a reversal at that system, and descriptors are ranked by the
median oriented AUC across systems minus its interquartile range, on up to
5{,}000 events per class of each system drawn at random.

\subsection{Transfer with no target labels}\label{subsec:transfer}

Each system is first classified with all its classes above the support
criterion, global and local, by a logistic regression with a fixed
regularization strength and by gradient-boosted trees with a fixed
configuration, the same at every system, scored by balanced accuracy over
five-fold cross-validation repeated five times on up to 300 events per class.

Transfer is measured by training a classifier on a single source system and
scoring it on another, and the scores of all source--target pairs form a
matrix. The main matrix holds LP and VT. Its sources are the systems with
both classes above the support criterion, the five OVDAS systems, the four
VCSEIS regions and Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, and its targets are every
system with LP or VT above that criterion, including those with a single
class: the six VOISS-Net volcanoes, Nevado del Ruiz at Bis and the 2009
Deception Island deployment. Its pairs change the volcano within one labeling
agency, among the OVDAS systems, or the agency that draws the labels: OVDAS,
the SGC, each observatory behind a VCSEIS region, the VOISS-Net team and the
Deception Island team. A second matrix adds HB, the class that Nevado del
Ruiz shares with the OVDAS taxonomy; its sources are Copahue, Hudson and
Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, and Nevado del Ruiz at Bis enters as a target
scored on its LP and HB events. In both matrices the diagonal, the source
scored on its own events left out of each training draw, is the reference,
and the difference between a transfer score and the diagonal score of its
target is the transfer loss. Each step, the same agency or another agency,
is summarized by the median over its pairs.

Two classifiers are trained on each source. Gradient-boosted decision trees
\cite{chen_xgboost_2016} use the descriptors at their native scales, since a
tree ensemble is invariant to monotone rescaling, and an $L_2$-regularized
logistic regression uses them standardized with the mean and variance of its
training draw. Each training set holds 100 events per class drawn at random
from the source, a size that every class of every source exceeds, which keeps
sources of very different volume comparable and the class prior of the
source out of the decision. The draw is repeated 20 times, and each score is
reported as the mean of the repetitions. The matrices use, at Copahue, a
fixed random set of 60{,}000 events.

Hyperparameters are tuned on the source alone, on a separate draw of 100
events per class that may share events with the training draws and the
diagonal. For the boosted trees, a tree-structured
Parzen estimator \cite{akiba_optuna_2019} runs 200 trials that maximize the
balanced accuracy of stratified five-fold cross-validation on that draw, over
boosting rounds in $[200, 2000]$, maximum depth in $[3, 12]$, learning rate
in $[0.01, 0.3]$, row and column subsampling in $[0.5, 1.0]$, minimum child
weight in $[1, 50]$, and $L_1$ and $L_2$ regularization in $[10^{-8}, 10]$,
with the learning rate, child weight and both regularization terms sampled
log-uniformly. For the logistic regression, the same cross-validation
selects $C$ among 13 log-spaced values between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{3}$. The
configuration of a source stays fixed across its 20 draws and all its
targets.

Each target is scored by the recall of every class it holds and, when it
holds all the classes of the matrix, by their geometric mean,
\begin{equation}
G_m = \left( \prod_{c=1}^{C} \mathrm{recall}_c \right)^{1/C},
\end{equation}
which rewards recovering every class regardless of their proportion in the
target. Scores are computed on up to 5{,}000 events per class of the target,
drawn at random. The permutation importance of each descriptor, the drop in
balanced accuracy averaged over three permutations, is computed on every
source--target pair, the diagonal included, over the first three draws and
up to 1{,}000 events of each target class above the support criterion.

\subsection{Label budget}\label{subsec:budget}

The label budget measures how many labeled target events bring a transferred
model to the level of a model trained on the target. Each system with LP and
VT above the support criterion is a target in turn, and each of the other
nine is its source. In each of three repetitions, the target is split into a
fixed test set, half of each class up to 300 events, and a pool that keeps
the natural composition of the target. The source contributes 100 events per
class, and $n$ labeled target events, with $n$ in $\{0, 10, 20, 50, 100,
200\}$ in total rather than per class, are added from the pool in two ways:
at random, or by active learning, which starts from the source model and, in
batches of 10, labels the pool events with the smallest margin
$|p(\mathrm{LP}) - 0.5|$ and retrains, the uncertainty sampling studied for
volcano-seismic events by \cite{bueno_continuous_2022,manley_deep_2022}. Two references bound the curve: the
same model trained on the $n$ random target events alone, and the local
model, trained on up to 100 events per class of the pool.

Three classifiers enter the budget. The logistic regression and the boosted
trees keep the configuration tuned for each source in the transfer matrix
and use balanced class weights; the target-only and local models take the
configuration tuned for the target, and below 200 events the target-only
boosted trees take a fixed configuration for small samples, 200 rounds of
depth 3, a learning rate of 0.1 and no minimum child weight or $L_1$ term,
since the tuned ones cannot split a node on so few events. TabPFN
\cite{hollmann_accurate_2025}, a transformer pretrained on synthetic tabular
tasks that classifies a test set in context from the training set without
gradient updates, enters with its default configuration and an ensemble of
four, with random selection only. Every model is scored by $G_m$ on the fixed
test set, and the curve of each target is the median over its sources of the
mean over repetitions.

\subsection{Automatic segmentation}\label{subsec:autocut}

The validation events are also cut with the automatic proposal of
Section~\ref{subsec:manualcut} alone, its onset and end of the coda, and
their sixteen descriptors recomputed with the same code. Both cuts cover the
same LP and VT events. The comparison measures the shift of the onset and of
the end of the coda, the share of proposals the analyst accepted unchanged,
the rank correlation of $FI$, $DET$ and $SR$ between the cuts, and the change
in score within each system, by five-fold cross-validation over five draws of
up to 100 events per class with the folds paired between the cuts, and as a
transfer target of the five OVDAS sources, whose events keep their catalog
onset, with the configurations and the 20 draws of the transfer matrix.

\subsection{Checks of the transfer design}\label{subsec:checks}

Two checks probe the training draw and the class prior of the LP--VT matrix.
In the first, the 100 events per class of each source are drawn
systematically in time, one every $N/100$ events of the class in time order
from a random offset, instead of at random, with the same seed for the model,
so that a source catalog spanning years enters with its whole history. In the
second, each target is scored with all its LP and VT events, in its natural
composition, and the share of LP is estimated without its labels by black-box
shift estimation \cite{lipton_detecting_2018}, from the confusion matrix of
the source under five-fold cross-validation, and by the
expectation--maximization of \cite{saerens_adjusting_2002} on
Platt-calibrated probabilities. The calibrated probabilities are reweighted
by the estimated prior and the event is assigned to the most probable class;
the reweighting with the true prior bounds what a perfect estimate would
give.

\subsection{Audit of the expert labels}\label{subsec:audit}

Confident learning \cite{northcutt_confident_2021} flags the events whose
catalog label disagrees with the rest of the dataset. Within each system,
with every class of at least 50 events and at most 20{,}000 drawn at random
per class, out-of-sample probabilities from five-fold cross-validation of
gradient-boosted trees with a fixed configuration (300 rounds of depth 6, a
learning rate of 0.1 and subsampling of 0.8) and of a logistic regression
($C = 1$) on the sixteen descriptors, scaled with the median and
interquartile range of the system, estimate the joint distribution of given
and true labels, and the events whose label falls in an off-diagonal cell,
pruned by the estimated noise rate of each class, are flagged as suspect.

Across datasets, the same trees, with balanced class weights, are trained on
up to 1{,}000 events per class of a source and predict up to 2{,}000 events
per class of a target, over the classes among LP, VT and HB that both hold
above the support criterion, for every pair with at least two shared classes,
in three repetitions. The share of target labels flagged with these
probabilities is compared with the share flagged with the out-of-sample
probabilities of the target itself on the same events. The per-class
thresholds of confident learning absorb a shift of all the probabilities, so
an excess of flags over the own criterion that appears in one class and not
in the others points to a difference in how that class is labeled.

Unsupervised clustering tests whether the labeled classes form dense groups
in descriptor space. Within each system, HDBSCAN
\cite{campello_density-based_2013} and, as a reference, DBSCAN
\cite{ester_density-based_1996} group the
sixteen scaled descriptors reduced by principal components to 90\% of their
variance, on a random subsample of up to 4{,}000 events drawn regardless of
class. Their parameters are chosen with 80 trials of a tree-structured Parzen
estimator that maximize the density-based clustering validation index (DBCV)
\cite{moulavi_density-based_2014}, so the labels take no part in the fit; the
agreement with the labels is measured afterwards by the adjusted Rand index,
and the DBCV of the expert labels themselves, in the same space, measures
whether the classes form dense, separated groups.

At Hudson, an analyst reviews the 200 events with the lowest label quality
among those flagged by both models, seeing the trace and the spectrogram with
the catalog class and the suggested class, and confirms or relabels each one.
Since HB at OVDAS is told from LP by an impulsive, high-frequency onset, the
share of energy above 4.5~Hz in the first 2~s after the onset,
$HF_{\text{onset}}$, is measured on up to 2{,}000 HB and 2{,}000 LP events
drawn evenly across deciles of signal-to-noise ratio at Hudson and Copahue, and on every HB
and LP event of Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta and Bis from the manual onset, and
it is compared between HB and LP within each quartile of signal-to-noise
ratio.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Transfer with no target labels}\label{subsec:res_transfer}

Within each system, with all their classes, the descriptors separate the
taxonomy to a degree that falls with the number and the overlap of its
classes (Figure~\ref{fig:confusion}, Appendix~\ref{secA1}). With the boosted
trees, the balanced accuracy is 0.97 at Laguna del Maule, 0.83 to 0.94 at
the three VCSEIS regions with two classes, 0.92 at Deception Island in 2009
and 0.80 at Veniaminof, also with two; 0.81 at Villarrica with three and 0.75
at Nevados de Chill\'an with five; 0.62 to 0.67 at Copahue, Hudson and both
stations of Nevado del Ruiz, which hold HB; 0.53 in Alaska with four; and
0.45 and 0.51 at Pavlof and Semisopochnoi with six, against chance levels of
0.17 to 0.50.

Six descriptors keep the direction of their contrast between LP and VT at
all ten systems that hold both classes: $FI$, $DET$, $SR$,
$f_{\text{peak}}$, $W$ and $W_{entr}$ (Table~\ref{tab:transferability} and
Figure~\ref{fig:aucsystems}, Appendix~\ref{secA1}). $FI$ leads the ranking with a median oriented AUC of
0.93, lowest in Alaska (0.73), followed by $DET$ and $SR$ (0.87); the other
recurrence descriptors reach 0.59 to 0.76. The transferred models rely on
the same descriptors: permuting $FI$ costs the boosted trees the most
balanced accuracy from seven of the ten LP--VT sources, 8 to 31 percentage
points as a median over targets, and $FI$, $DET$ and $SR$ are the three most
important descriptors with both classifiers (Figure~\ref{fig:importance},
Appendix~\ref{secA1}).

The diagonal of the LP--VT matrix reaches a median $G_m$ of 0.90 to 0.91,
lowest at Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, Hawai`i and Alaska and highest at
Laguna del Maule (Figure~\ref{fig:matrices}); here and below, a range spans
the two classifiers. Moving to another volcano of the same agency lowers the
median $G_m$ per pair to 0.82 to 0.85, and moving to another agency to 0.80
to 0.82, a median transfer loss of 0.07 to 0.09 in both steps, so a change of
labeling agency costs no more than a change of volcano. Over all sources,
the recall of LP at the VOISS-Net volcanoes and at the 2009 Deception Island
deployment is 0.92 to 0.99, and that of VT 0.87 to 0.88 at Tanaga, 0.74 to
0.75 at K\={\i}lauea and 0.65 to 0.66 at Redoubt. Nevado del Ruiz at Bis is
the exception: the median recall of its LP over all sources is 0.03 to 0.05.

HB loses most of its recall between systems: from 0.57 to 0.61 within its
system, it falls to 0.15 to 0.16 between Copahue and Hudson, to 0.19 to 0.20
between agencies and to 0.23 to 0.35 from Olleta to Bis. The $G_m$ of the
three classes is 0.68 to 0.69 within each system, 0.36 to 0.37 between

Nevado del Ruiz its LP collapses as well, to recalls of 0.002 to 0.077 from

\subsection{Label budget}\label{subsec:res_budget}

The targets differ most in where they start (Figure~\ref{fig:budget}). With
no target labels, the logistic regression reaches a $G_m$ of 0.84 to 0.89 at
Nevados de Chill\'an, the Cascade Range, Laguna del Maule, Northern
California and Villarrica, within 0.02 to 0.04 of the local model at the
first two and 0.07 to 0.10 below it at the other three, and of 0.76 to 0.82 at Copahue, Hawai`i and Alaska, against local models
of 0.83 to 0.87. Hudson starts at 0.73 against 0.94, and Nevado del Ruiz at
Olleta at 0.45 against 0.75 to 0.85.

About 100 target labels bring the transferred models to the local one. At
Hudson, random labels lift the logistic regression from 0.73 to 0.81 at 20
and 0.90 at 100 labels, and at Copahue, Hawai`i and Alaska 100 to 200 labels
bring every classifier within 0.03 of the local model. Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta stays below its local
model, at 0.65 to 0.71 with 100 labels, and there the source hinders: TabPFN
trained on the target events alone reaches 0.63 with 20 and 0.81 with 100,
above every model that adds them to the source.

TabPFN gains the most where the transfer falls short. At Hudson it starts at
0.78 and reaches 0.88 with 20 random labels and 0.93 with 50, against 0.81 and
0.85 for the logistic regression, and trained on 10 target events alone it
already reaches 0.91; at the targets that start near their local model the
three classifiers lie within 0.05 of each other. Active learning adds
0.01 to 0.03 over random selection to the logistic regression at most
targets, and up to 0.06 to the boosted trees at Laguna del Maule with 100
labels.

Strongly imbalanced targets defeat both selections. Villarrica holds 31{,}483
LP and 308 VT events, so a random draw from its pool rarely holds a VT: the
target-only models stay at $G_m = 0$ up to 200 labels, and added to the

TabPFN from 0.84 to 0.74 and active learning from 0.84 to 0.75 to 0.78,
against local models of 0.93 to 0.94 trained on balanced classes. At Laguna
del Maule, with 445 LP and 4{,}616 VT events, the target-only models need 50
to 200 labels to approach the transferred ones.

\subsection{Automatic segmentation}\label{subsec:res_autocut}

Where the catalog gives a reference, the automatic cut leaves the descriptors
and the scores unchanged. At the four VCSEIS regions and the two Deception
Island deployments, the analyst accepted 37\% to 81\% of the proposals
unchanged, the onset moves by more than 1~s in 2\% to 5\% of the events, and
35\% at Deception Island in 2009, and the rank correlation of $FI$, $DET$
and $SR$ between the cuts is 0.96 to 1.00. Within each VCSEIS region the
$G_m$ changes by at most 0.01, and as a transfer target by less than 0.01.
At Nevado del Ruiz, whose files carry no reference, the analyst accepted 0\%
to 4\% of the proposals, the correlation falls to 0.78 to 0.93, and the
$G_m$ within Olleta drops by 0.01 to 0.02.

The VOISS-Net windows, labeled spans of 120~s with no arrival, are where the
cut matters. The analyst accepted 27\% to 53\% of the proposals, the onset
moves by more than 1~s in 40\% to 57\% of the events, and the correlation of
the three descriptors falls to 0.36 to 0.79. As a transfer target, the recall
of VT falls with the automatic cut from 0.72 to 0.58 at K\={\i}lauea with the
logistic regression and from 0.78 to 0.61 with the boosted trees, and from
0.81 to 0.73 and 0.88 to 0.78 at Tanaga, while at Redoubt it rises from 0.70
to 0.81 and 0.73 to 0.82, and the recall of LP at Veniaminof falls by 0.03.

\subsection{Checks of the transfer design}\label{subsec:res_checks}

Drawing the training events of each source evenly in time leaves the transfer
unchanged: the median change in $G_m$ per target is within 0.01 for both
classifiers, and 4 of the 180 source--target pairs change by more than 0.05,
in either direction.

Correcting the prior of the target, estimated without its labels, raises the
accuracy at the single-class targets and lowers $G_m$ wherever both classes
are present. At Redoubt the accuracy rises from 0.61 to 0.63 to 0.76 to 0.81,
at K\={\i}lauea from 0.72 to 0.74 to 0.80 to 0.86 and at Tanaga from 0.87 to
0.89 to 0.99 to 1.00. Where both classes are present, the estimate of the LP
share misses by a median of 0.01 to 0.13 at the targets near their diagonal
and by 0.36 to 0.45 at Hudson and Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, and the
reweighting lowers $G_m$ by up to 0.05 where the prior is near balance, from 0.78 to 0.84
to 0.71 to 0.75 at Copahue, from 0.85 to 0.87 to 0.45 to 0.46 at Laguna del
Maule, to 0 at Villarrica, and from 0.66 to 0.67 to 0.35 to 0.42 at Hudson.

Copahue, HB of Hudson and Nevado del Ruiz at Bis, and the regional and other
events of Alaska. Three global classes stand out: VT at Villarrica
(52\%), with 308 events against 31{,}483 LP, LP at Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta
(53\%) and VT in Alaska (39\%). The balanced accuracy of the
out-of-sample models follows the same order, from 0.91 to 0.92 at Laguna del
Maule and Deception Island in 2009 to 0.53 in Alaska.

10\% to 36\% with their own criterion; at Bis, whose 70 HB events the own
criterion already flags at 47\%, another source flags 28\%. The disagreement holds within one agency: with
the criterion of Hudson, 81\% of the HB of Copahue are flagged, most of them
as VT, and with the criterion of Copahue, 69\% of the HB of Hudson, most as
LP; from Hudson and Copahue, 56\% to 84\% of the HB of Nevado del Ruiz at
Olleta are flagged as VT. With HB among the classes, 45\% to 87\% of the VT of
Hudson are flagged, most of them as HB, by the criteria of Copahue and
Olleta.

Without labels, the descriptors form no group that matches the classes.
HDBSCAN retains two groups at every system, with an adjusted Rand index of
$-0.07$ to 0.08 with the labels, and the DBCV of the expert labels is
negative at all nine systems, $-0.72$ to $-0.85$, so the classes do not form
dense, separated groups in descriptor space.

At Hudson, the review of the 200 suspects changed 141 labels. Of the 102 HB
events, 31 stayed HB, 24 became LP, 29 VT and 18 AV. The HB relabeled as LP
carry a median $HF_{\text{onset}}$ of 0.20, against 0.50 for those kept as
HB. Over the catalog, the high-frequency onset sets HB apart from LP at both
OVDAS systems and not at Nevado del Ruiz. Within each quartile of
signal-to-noise ratio, the median $HF_{\text{onset}}$ of HB is 2.7 to 4.5
times that of LP at Hudson and 2.1 to 2.8 times at Copahue, while at Olleta
and Bis both classes lie between 0.58 and 0.81. At Hudson, the share of HB
among HB and LP events rises from 1.1\% in the lowest quintile of
signal-to-noise ratio to 14.3\% in the highest.

\subsection{Coda-length window}\label{subsec:codawindow}

Many events outlast the 10~s window (Table~\ref{tab:durations}), which then
measures their onset and early coda. A second window follows each event to
the end of its coda, from 5~s, the shortest record that supports the
recurrence descriptors, to 100~s, after which a long coda is measured on its
first 100~s, as in the full-duration window of \cite{morales_recurrence_2026}.
The duration is the analyst-assigned one at the OVDAS systems and the end of
the coda set by hand at the validation systems. The window is cut from a
segment padded from 10~s before the onset to 3~s after its end and
conditioned as the fixed-length window, with a 5~s noise window from 7 to 2~s
before the onset (Figure~\ref{fig:codawindow}). At Laguna del Maule, Nevados
de Chill\'an, Copahue and Villarrica it is computed on a subsample of the
events lasting 5~s or more, in which every class above the support criterion
enters in full up to 10 times, or 100 times at Villarrica, the size of the
smallest class, and a larger class contributes that many events taken at even
steps in time; Hudson enters in full. The descriptors and the transfer design
are those of the main text.

With this window, the diagonal of the LP--VT matrix reaches a median $G_m$ of
0.90 to 0.91 and the pairs outside it 0.78 to 0.83, and the two classifiers
differ by at most 0.05 at every step. $FI$ leads the ranking of
Table~\ref{tab:transferability} with a median AUC of 0.94, $SR$ rises to
second place (0.89) and $DET$ falls to 0.79 and reverses at Northern
California.
