\abstract{Automatic classifiers of volcano-seismic events reach high accuracy on
the catalog they are trained on and lose it on the next dataset, so every
new volcano, station or deployment is labeled again by an expert. This
study measures how many expert labels a new dataset needs and whether
recent methods lower that number, on twenty datasets of labeled events
from five catalogs and eight labeling agencies, with sixteen interpretable descriptors of a 10~s
window. A classifier trained on one dataset is applied to every other with
no target labels, then with a budget of 10 to 200 target labels chosen at
random, stratified or by active learning, with a logistic regression,
gradient-boosted trees and the tabular foundation model TabPFN, against a
model trained on the target alone and a local model; the manual
segmentation is compared with an automatic one; the training draw and the
class prior are checked with label-free estimators; and the labels are
audited with confident learning within and across datasets, with
unsupervised clustering and with a manual review. LP and VT transfer with
a loss of 0.07 to 0.09 in $G_m$ whether the volcano or the labeling agency
changes, and about 100 stratified labels bring a transferred model within
0.03 of the local one at eight of ten targets; TabPFN reaches with 20
labels what the tuned classifiers reach with 100 where the transfer starts
low, active learning adds 0.01 to 0.03, and label-shift correction lowers
$G_m$ wherever both classes are present. The hybrid class HB does not
transfer, even between two datasets of one observatory: another dataset's
criterion flags 40\% to 70\% of its labels against 6\% to 14\% for LP and VT,
no unsupervised grouping reproduces any class, and the feature the analysts
use for HB, a high-frequency onset, scales with the energy of the event.
The label budget of a new dataset is thus set by the transfer it starts
from, and its residual cost lies in the definition of the local classes
rather than in the classifier.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Volcano observatories rely on catalogs of classified seismic events to read
the state of a volcano, and analysts classify each event as long-period
(LP), volcano-tectonic (VT), hybrid (HB) or another class from its waveform
and spectrum \cite{mcnutt_volcanic_2015,chouet_volcano_2003}. Automatic
classifiers trained on these catalogs have moved from hand-crafted features
with classical classifiers, such as support vector machines, random forests
and Gaussian mixture models \cite{malfante_automatic_2018,venegas_combining_2019},
to convolutional and recurrent networks on spectrograms and waveforms
\cite{canario_-depth_2020,lara_deep_2021,titos_toward_2022,titos_dilated-rnns_2024}.
They reach accuracies above 90\% on the catalog they were trained on
\cite{morales_artificial_2026}.

That accuracy is measured within one catalog. A classifier trained at one
volcano and applied to another, to another station or to a catalog labeled
by another observatory meets a different source population, a different
path and site response and a different labeling practice. Classification
systems lose accuracy when moved to another station, eruptive scenario or
volcano \cite{cortes_practical_2021} and as a volcano evolves over the
years \cite{castro-cabrera_adaptive_2021}. Multi-volcano training sets
\cite{fee_generalized_2025}, multi-station inputs
\cite{salazar_multi-station_2023} and transfer learning
\cite{bueno_volcano-seismic_2020} reduce the loss but do not remove it. The
systematic review of the field \cite{morales_artificial_2026} finds that few
studies test transferability between volcanoes and that inconsistent
labeling across catalogs, together with scarce open datasets, hinders
generalization. In practice, a new volcano, station or deployment is
labeled again by an expert before a classifier serves it
\cite{cortes_practical_2021}.

The labels themselves are the second problem. A catalog entry is an
operational decision, often taken under the time pressure of unrest, and
the labeling criteria, even the names of the classes, differ between
operators and between catalogs
\cite{cortes_practical_2021,titos_could_2025}. Catalogs can be audited for
mislabeled events, with a blind review by observatory scientists
\cite{anzieta_cleaning_2023}, and completed or corrected by a model trained
on another catalog \cite{titos_could_2025}, whose recognition varies widely
when the labeling criteria of the two catalogs differ. Waveforms can in turn
be standardized before classification to reduce the differences between
sensors and sites \cite{cortes_standardization_2019}. These methods act on
the labels or on the recording, but none separates the two causes of a
transfer loss: a classifier that fails to carry a class across datasets,
and a class that is defined differently in each dataset.

Several methods promise to cut the number of labels a new dataset needs.
Transfer learning reuses a model trained elsewhere
\cite{titos_classification_2020,bueno_volcano-seismic_2020}; active learning
chooses which events the expert labels, by uncertainty
\cite{bueno_continuous_2022} or by diversity \cite{manley_deep_2022};
unsupervised methods group unlabeled events or learn representations from
them \cite{duque_exploring_2020,rios_learning_2024}; a foundation model
pretrained on synthetic tabular tasks adapts to small labeled sets
\cite{hollmann_accurate_2025}; label-shift estimators adapt a classifier to
the class proportions of the target without its labels
\cite{lipton_detecting_2018,saerens_adjusting_2002}; and confident learning
locates the labels that disagree with the rest of a dataset
\cite{northcutt_confident_2021}. Each has been evaluated on its own terms,
and the active-learning studies weigh labels against performance within one
volcano \cite{bueno_continuous_2022,manley_deep_2022}. What is missing is a
common measurement across datasets: how many expert labels a new dataset
needs to reach the performance of a model trained on it, which of these
methods lowers that number, and how much of the remaining gap comes from the
labels rather than from the classifier.

This study makes that measurement. The question is one of data rather than
of volcanoes: a labeled dataset, its shift with respect to another, the
budget of labels that closes the shift, and the criteria of the annotators
who produced the labels. Volcano seismology is the case, with twenty
datasets of labeled events from five catalogs, five from the catalog of the
Southern Andes Volcanological Observatory (OVDAS) and fifteen from four
public or shared catalogs, which together span volcanoes, stations,
deployments and eight labeling agencies. Each event is described by sixteen
interpretable descriptors of a 10~s window: thirteen from recurrence
quantification analysis, with the embedding a previous study selected on
the OVDAS catalog \cite{morales_recurrence_2026}, and three from the
spectrum \cite{cui_subdivision_2021}. Their small number keeps every model
trainable from a hundred events and every result readable in terms of named
features.

The analysis proceeds in five steps. A classifier trained on one dataset is
applied to another with no labels of the target, over every pair of datasets
that share LP or VT, to establish what transfers and what does not. A budget of target
labels is then added, from ten to two hundred, with three classifiers and three ways of choosing
the labels, random, stratified and active, and two references, a model
trained on the target labels alone and the local model, to measure how many
labels close the gap and which method lowers the number. The segmentation
of the events by hand is compared with an automatic one, to locate the cost
of a new dataset between its onsets and its labels. Two checks probe the training draw of the source
and, with estimators that need no labels, the class prior of the target. The labels themselves are audited with confident learning within
each dataset and across datasets, with unsupervised clustering on the
descriptors and on the amplitude spectrum, with a manual review of the
suspects at one volcano and with a direct measurement of the feature that
the OVDAS analysts use to separate HB from LP. Section~\ref{sec:data}
describes the datasets, Section~\ref{sec:methods} the descriptors and the
five analyses, Section~\ref{sec:results} their results and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} what they imply for adding a dataset to an
automatic classifier.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

The study draws on twenty datasets of labeled volcano-seismic events: five
from the catalog of one observatory, OVDAS, and fifteen from four catalogs
compiled by other agencies. Each dataset is one volcano, region, station or
deployment of one catalog, labeled under one practice,
so that transferring a classifier between two of them changes the volcano,
the recording or the labeling agency. This section describes the OVDAS
catalog first and the four validation catalogs after it.

\subsection{Volcanoes and monitoring network}\label{subsec:area}

OVDAS monitors the active volcanoes of southern Chile within the
National Geology and Mining Service (SERNAGEOMIN) \cite{amigo_volcano_2021}
and keeps a continuous event catalog for the local seismic network of each
volcano. Five volcanoes were selected for their large event volumes, their
joint coverage of the classes of interest and their distinct volcanic
environments.

These environments differ in the processes that drive their seismicity.
Villarrica is an open-conduit system with an active lava lake and
near-continuous long-period (LP) seismicity \cite{richardson_varying_2014}.
Nevados de Chill\'an has erupted since 2016, alternating effusive and
explosive phases \cite{cardona_volcanic_2021}. Copahue holds a hot acidic
crater lake over a shallow hydrothermal system, in an eruptive cycle that
began in 2012 \cite{carbajal_seasonal_2025}. At Laguna del Maule,
volcano-tectonic (VT) seismicity accompanies the inflation of its
magmatic-hydrothermal system \cite{cardona_crustal_2018}. Hudson is a heavily
glaciated stratovolcano near the Chile Triple Junction
\cite{agurto-detzel_seismicity_2014}, and its catalog combines VT, LP and
avalanche events (Table~\ref{tab:counts}).

The five volcanoes span about 1100~km of the Southern Volcanic Zone
(Figure~\ref{fig:map}), and each contributes the vertical channel of one
station, sampled at 100~Hz, over the interval covered by its analyzed events
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}). The records differ between datasets in instrument
response, source--station distance, site conditions and event population. The intervals differ as well: Laguna del Maule starts in
April 2021, after station NI2Z began operating on
30 March 2021. Copahue holds 99.1\% of its analyzed events between 2013 and
2016, and its station detects 979 events from 2017 to its last event in
August 2024. Hudson has 28 events in March 2013 and a record from June 2013
to January 2024 that includes months without events.

\subsection{Nature of the catalog}\label{subsec:catalog}

Each catalog entry is an operational classification by an analyst, the
expert labeler of this study. The analyst assigns the event class, the
detecting station, an onset at that station and a duration. The median
duration of a class differs between datasets (Table~\ref{tab:durations}),
with the event population and with the practice of each catalog: LP lasts
11.0~s at Copahue and 29.7~s at Villarrica, and VT 8.0~s at Laguna del Maule
and 17.9~s at Villarrica. Most events thus outlast the 10~s window of this
study (Section~\ref{subsec:conditioning}), which measures their onset and
early coda.

\subsection{Event classes}\label{subsec:classes}

The analysis uses six operational classes of the OVDAS catalog, based on
the classifications of \cite{lahr_earthquake_1994} and
\cite{chouet_volcano_2003}: LP (long-period), VT (volcano-tectonic), HB
(hybrid), VLP (very-long-period, coded LV), AV (avalanche and rockfall) and
TO (tornillo). They account for 63--97\% of the catalog entries of each
dataset between 2013 and 2024 (Table~\ref{tab:rawcounts}). All six are
discrete events, which gives the analysis a common unit of observation. The
remaining codes describe sustained tremor (TR), observational attributes,
detections below the classification threshold and further operational
codes.

Analysts assign the six classes from waveform morphology and spectral
content (Figure~\ref{fig:gallery}), so the classes are operational signal
categories, and the interpretations below are those commonly attached to
each label. LP events show emergent onsets and narrow-band low-frequency
energy, usually attributed to resonance in fluid-filled cracks
\cite{chouet_long-period_1996}. VT events show impulsive onsets and
broadband energy extending to higher frequencies, consistent with brittle
rock failure \cite{white_volcano-tectonic_2016}. HB events combine an
impulsive onset with a low-frequency coda
\cite{lahr_earthquake_1994,harrington_volcanic_2007}. VLP events carry
energy at lower frequencies than LP and are linked to slow fluid transport
or conduit deformation \cite{almendros_identifying_2002}. AV events are
produced by avalanches on the edifice \cite{ferreira_deep_2023} and appear
as emergent, broadband and long signals, and TO events show slowly
decaying, quasi-monochromatic codas \cite{gomez_m_tornillo_1999}. The classes overlap in waveform and spectral
features, and HB in particular lies between LP and VT.

Class composition varies strongly between datasets. LP makes up 99.3\% of the

\subsection{Validation catalogs}\label{subsec:validation}

Four catalogs compiled by other agencies provide labeled records from other
volcanoes, networks and labeling practices (Table~\ref{tab:validation}), and
the study uses every class each catalog labels. LP and VT, present in all
four catalogs and at every OVDAS dataset, are the global labels. The classes
particular to one catalog or one observatory are the local labels: HB, TO,
VLP and AV at the OVDAS datasets and the other classes of
Table~\ref{tab:validation}. The events analyzed at each validation dataset are given in
Table~\ref{tab:valcounts} (Appendix~\ref{subsec:supp_valcounts}).

The Deception Island catalog
\cite{cortes_standardization_2019,cortes_moreno_vsr_2018} holds two deployments on this Antarctic volcano,
recorded at 50~Hz: a short-period sensor between the Argentinean and Spanish
bases in 1995 and a broadband station near the Spanish base in 2009. Its
label file gives the start and end of every event, often several per record,
as the volcano produces swarms. The study uses every LP, VT and NTR event,
the catalog's class for noise and tremor signals, and each deployment forms a
dataset of its own.

VCSEIS \cite{zhong_deep-learning-based_2024}, distributed in SeisBench format
\cite{woollam_seisbenchtoolbox_2022}, covers four regions catalogued by the Alaska
Volcano Observatory, the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, the Northern
California Earthquake Data Center and the Pacific Northwest Seismic Network
(Cascade Range). Each trace is sampled at 100~Hz, carries P and S arrivals and
takes its class from the catalog field that separates long-period from local
events; an earthquake recorded at several stations appears once per station.
The study draws at random 500 LP and 500 VT traces per region and up to 500
traces of each further Alaska class: regional earthquakes, tremor, avalanches
and other events.

The VOISS-Net training set \cite{fee_generalized_2025} labels 120~s windows
of continuous data at volcanoes in Alaska, Hawai`i, Mexico and Italy, one
label per window and network, and the study uses the volcanoes whose
waveforms the EarthScope data center serves, which leaves out Mt.~Etna. Its Earthquake and Long Period labels correspond to VT
and LP, and each volcano contributes one of them: VT at K\={\i}lauea,
Redoubt and Tanaga, LP at Veniaminof, Semisopochnoi and Pavlof. Its other
labels enter as local classes: harmonic, monochromatic and broadband tremor
at Pavlof and Semisopochnoi, harmonic tremor at Veniaminof and at
Popocat\'epetl, its only class, and explosions and noise at Pavlof and
Semisopochnoi. The study takes every labeled VT and LP window and up to 500
windows per volcano of each local class, drawn at random. The waveforms come
from the EarthScope data center, at the vertical channel of a reference
station per volcano or of the next station of a fixed list when it lacks the
window, from 60~s before to 120~s after each labeled window.

The Servicio Geol\'ogico Colombiano (SGC) provided 500 records per class,
labeled LP, VT, HB and tremor by its analysts. The letter R of the file
extension marks those from Nevado del Ruiz: 498 LP, 394 VT, 500 HB and 500
tremor, the last split into 250 of continuous tremor and 250 of tremor
pulses. Each record comes as one file with the stations available at the
time, sampled at a nominal 100~Hz, without catalog onset or duration. The SGC
designates Olleta as the reference station of the volcano and Bis as its
backup. The continuous-tremor files do not include Olleta, so continuous
tremor is recorded at Bis and tremor pulses at Olleta. At Olleta, 201 HB and
2 LP events come from OLLE, the single-component sensor of the older network,
taken as vertical, and the others from the vertical channel OLLZ.

The validation datasets also differ in their recording conditions
(Table~\ref{tab:recording}). At five VOISS-Net volcanoes the reference
station records 93\% or more of the LP or VT windows; at Pavlof it records
57\%, and PVV and PN7A record the rest. The Tanaga station also lies 4.7~km
from neighboring Takawangha. The tremor of Popocat\'epetl is recorded at
four stations, and ZACA samples at
40~Hz in 243 of its 272 windows. Semisopochnoi, Tanaga, K\={\i}lauea, the Hawai`i region and Deception
Island stand on islands, the Alaska region spans islands and continent, and
the other validation datasets, like the five OVDAS datasets, stand on a
continent. A transfer between two validation datasets thus changes the
recording along with the labeling agency.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methods}

The analysis turns each event into sixteen descriptors computed on a
fixed-length window of its waveform
(Sections~\ref{subsec:stationselection}--\ref{subsec:features}) and then
follows the order of the question. A classifier trained on one dataset, the
source, is applied to another, the target, with no target labels
(Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}) and then with a growing budget of them
(Section~\ref{subsec:budget}); the comparison is repeated with the validation
events segmented automatically (Section~\ref{subsec:autocut}); two checks
probe the training draw and the class prior (Section~\ref{subsec:checks});
and the labels themselves are audited within each dataset and across
datasets (Section~\ref{subsec:audit}). Every score is measured within one
dataset or one pair of datasets, and every summary across datasets is built
from those values.

\subsection{Station selection}\label{subsec:stationselection}

A dataset is the set of events of one catalog from one volcano, region,
station or deployment. Each OVDAS dataset uses one station, the one that combines the largest number
of catalogued detections with uninterrupted operation
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}), which keeps a single instrument response and
source--station geometry within each class. Each validation catalog is
divided into datasets following its own recording (Table~\ref{tab:recording}):
a VOISS-Net volcano gathers the windows of its reference station and of the
stations that replace it, a VCSEIS region the traces of all its stations, and
each SGC station and each Deception Island deployment forms a dataset of its
own.

\subsection{Signal window}\label{subsec:conditioning}

The window spans 10~s, 1{,}000 samples at 100~Hz, from the onset assigned
by the analyst of the catalog or set by hand
(Section~\ref{subsec:manualcut}). It is cut from a segment padded from 7~s before the onset to 13~s after
it. The segment is linearly detrended and band-pass filtered between 0.5 and
10~Hz with a zero-phase fourth-order Butterworth filter, and the 3~s margins
absorb the edge transient of the filter. The padded segment comes from one
gap-free trace of the selected station. An event with a gap, an interruption
or a date before the station was installed therefore falls outside the
analyzed population, the second condition of Table~\ref{tab:filters}, as
does an event whose filtered window has zero variance.

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of each event compares the signal window
with a
2~s noise window, from 4 to 2~s before the onset, on the same filtered
segment:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNR_{dB}} = 20\log_{10}\frac{\mathrm{RMS}(\text{signal window})}{\mathrm{RMS}(\text{noise window})}.
\end{equation}
The 2~s gap keeps an emergent arrival out of the noise window, and the band
of 0.5 to 10~Hz keeps out the secondary oceanic microseism, so the ratio
measures recording quality where the events radiate. The signal window is
then z-scored to zero mean and unit variance (Figure~\ref{fig:window}).

A second window that follows each event to the end of its coda serves as a
sensitivity check of the transfer matrices
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:codawindow}).

\subsection{Manual segmentation of the validation records}\label{subsec:manualcut}

The validation catalogs give labels but not the onset and end of the coda
that the windows need. One analyst therefore sets or confirms both for every
validation event, together with a pre-event noise window, under a single
protocol for the four catalogs (Figure~\ref{fig:manualcut}).

Each record is shown as its trace, band-passed between 0.5 and 10~Hz, and
its spectrogram up to 25~Hz, with the catalog reference and an automatic
proposal. The proposal is a complete segmentation in itself, which the
analyst accepts or corrects. Its onset is the catalog arrival or segment
start where one exists. Otherwise it is the first trigger of a recursive
STA/LTA detector (0.5 and 5~s windows, thresholds of 3 and 1, record
band-passed between 1 and 10~Hz) at least 5.5~s into the record, within the
labeled window or, for the SGC files, anywhere in the record. Its end of the
coda is the catalog segment end where one exists. Otherwise it is the point
where the envelope, smoothed over 1~s, falls below 1.2 times its pre-event
median for 2~s, with a proposed duration of at least 5~s. Its noise window
spans 12 to 2~s before the onset. The analyst places the onset at the first
arrival of the labeled event, the end of the coda where the signal returns
to the pre-event level, and the noise window right before the onset, clear
of other events where the record allows it. The SNR is measured as at the
OVDAS datasets, from the onset set by the analyst.

band-passed between 0.5 and 10~Hz and normalized. Dotted lines: reference

An event is kept when the analyst identifies the labeled event in the record
and its coda ends within the record, and it is discarded when no event is
visible in the catalog reference or when it lasts less than 5~s. The onset of
a kept event falls within the reference, from 2~s before its start, or, with
a flag, just outside it. Events marked as uncertain are kept. An event enters the analysis when its
record holds the padded segment of the window. At Nevado del Ruiz, events
are reviewed at Olleta and, when not visible there, at Bis, as the SGC
indicates. Olleta and Bis therefore differ both in the station and in the
events each records. VOISS-Net windows are reviewed in random order within each
volcano and class. An event the analyst relabels as a tornillo (TO) is
analyzed as LP, with a flag.

Each event is kept once. Copies of one earthquake at several VCSEIS
stations share a catalog source identifier, and copies of an event spanning
two consecutive VOISS-Net windows or SGC files have onsets less than 3~s
apart. Of these copies, the one with its onset inside the reference and the
highest ratio of the event to the manual noise window is kept; this choice
is the use of the manual noise window.

Records sampled at 50 or 40~Hz are resampled to 100~Hz by polyphase filtering
before any other filtering, so that the window and the sixteen descriptors
are computed with the same code and parameters as at the OVDAS datasets.

Explosions and the further VCSEIS classes, which carry a catalog arrival or a
distinct first motion, follow the same protocol. Tremor, noise and the
Deception Island NTR class are continuous signals with no onset, so their
window is centered on the labeled span, extended where it is shorter than
10~s, and they carry no SNR.

\subsection{Class support threshold}\label{subsec:classsupport}

A class enters the per-class analyses of a dataset when it holds at least 50
events there, a threshold that admits the local classes labeled in a single
dataset alongside the global ones. It is fixed before any analysis and
applies to every dataset-class combination of the OVDAS and validation
datasets; a combination below it stays in the analyzed population.

\subsection{RQA and spectral descriptors}\label{subsec:features}

The window yields sixteen descriptors, thirteen from recurrence
quantification analysis (RQA) and three from the spectrum, computed on the
signal window of one event.

The recurrence descriptors $DET$, $L$, $L_{max}$, $DIV$, $L_{entr}$, $LAM$,
$TT$, $V_{max}$, $V_{entr}$, $W$, $W_{max}$, $W_{div}$ and $W_{entr}$
\cite{marwan_recurrence_2007} are those defined by
\cite{morales_recurrence_2026}. They are computed on the z-scored window
with the embedding that work selected on the OVDAS catalog by classifier
performance: dimension 2, delay of 3 samples and Theiler correction of 1,
with diagonal, vertical and white vertical lines counted from a length of 2
samples (Figure~\ref{fig:rqa}). The radius $\varepsilon$ is set for each event from
the grid $\{0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$ as the first with a recurrence rate ($RR$)
between 0.01 and 0.10, or otherwise the one with $RR$ closest to 0.03. $DIV$
and $W_{div}$ are the reciprocals of $L_{max}$ and $W_{max}$, so each pair
orders the events identically; they are left missing when that length is
zero, and all thirteen descriptors are left missing when every radius fails.
Where a later step needs complete values, a missing descriptor takes the
median of the data that step trains or scales on; the boosted trees of
Section~\ref{subsec:transfer} take it as missing.

Three spectral descriptors complement this set. They come from the FFT of the
filtered window before z-scoring, over 1.0 to 8.0~Hz, where the filter passes
at least 89\% of the amplitude. $f_{\text{peak}}$ is the frequency of maximum
amplitude in that band, and $SR$ the ratio of mean to maximum amplitude
\cite{cui_subdivision_2021}, low for energy concentrated in a narrow band and
close to 1 for a flat spectrum. $FI$ compares two bands separated by a gap
\cite{cui_subdivision_2021,buurman_seismic_2010}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FI} = \log_{10}\frac{\overline{A}(4.5\text{--}8.0~\mathrm{Hz})}{\overline{A}(1.0\text{--}2.5~\mathrm{Hz})},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{A}$ is the mean spectral amplitude of each band. One band applies to every event, which keeps the descriptors comparable.
$f_{\text{peak}}$ therefore measures the dominant frequency of the classes
that radiate within the band, LP and TO and most HB and AV events, and
bounds it for the others: it lies above the dominant frequency of VLP, which
radiates mostly below 1~Hz, and at or below that of VT, which often exceeds
8~Hz (Figure~\ref{fig:spectral}).

Beyond their definition, the descriptors are ranked by how far each one
carries the global classes across datasets. A descriptor is transferable when
it separates the global classes in the same direction and with a similar
strength in every dataset. Each descriptor is scored in each dataset holding LP and VT above the
support threshold by the area under the ROC curve (AUC) of LP against VT,
on up to 5{,}000 events per class drawn at random; the AUC needs no
classifier and no threshold. The AUC is oriented by its median direction
across datasets, so a value below 0.5 marks a reversal in that dataset.
Descriptors are ranked by the median oriented AUC across datasets minus its
interquartile range.
$FI$ and $DET$, the leading spectral and the leading recurrence descriptor,
define a plane in which the global and local classes of all datasets are
compared: LP and VT are drawn as densities over all datasets, with equal
weight for every dataset, and each class of each dataset with at least 50
events by its median, with its interquartile range for the local classes.

\subsection{Transfer with no target labels}\label{subsec:transfer}

Before any transfer, each dataset is classified with all its classes above
the support threshold, global and local, which measures how far the
descriptors separate each taxonomy on its own. Two classifiers enter: a
logistic regression and gradient-boosted decision trees
\cite{chen_xgboost_2016}, XGBoost, hereafter the boosted trees. Both keep
one fixed configuration in every dataset and are scored by balanced
accuracy over five-fold cross-validation repeated five times on up to 300
events per class.

Transfer is measured by training a classifier on a single source dataset
and scoring it on another, and the scores of all source--target pairs form
a matrix. The main matrix holds LP and VT. Its sources are the ten datasets
with both classes above the support threshold: the five OVDAS datasets, the
four VCSEIS regions and Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta. Its targets are every
dataset with LP or VT above that threshold, including those with a single
class: the six VOISS-Net volcanoes, Nevado del Ruiz at Bis and the 2009
Deception Island deployment. Each pair changes either the volcano within one
labeling agency, as between two OVDAS datasets, or the agency that draws the
labels: OVDAS, the SGC, each observatory behind a VCSEIS region, the
VOISS-Net team and the Deception Island team.

A second matrix adds HB, the class that Nevado del
Ruiz shares with the OVDAS taxonomy; its sources are Copahue, Hudson and
Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, and Nevado del Ruiz at Bis enters as a target
scored on its LP and HB events. In both matrices the diagonal, the source scored on its own events left
out of each training draw, is the reference. The difference between a
transfer score and the diagonal score of its target is the transfer loss. Each step, another station of the same volcano,
another volcano of the same agency, or another agency, is summarized by the
median over its pairs.

The same two classifiers are trained on each source. The boosted trees use
the descriptors at their native scales, since a tree ensemble is invariant to
monotone rescaling, and the $L_2$-regularized
logistic regression uses them standardized with the mean and variance of its
training draw. Each training set holds 100 events per class drawn at random from the
source, a size that every class of every source exceeds. The fixed size
keeps sources of very different volume comparable and keeps the class prior
of the source out of the decision. The draw is repeated 20 times, and each
score is reported as the mean of the repetitions. The matrices use the
events that also hold the coda-length window of
Appendix~\ref{subsec:codawindow}; at Laguna del Maule, Nevados de
Chill\'an, Copahue and Villarrica these form a subsample stratified by
class, and at Copahue a fixed random set of 60{,}000 of them enters.

Hyperparameters are tuned on the source alone, on a separate draw of 100
events per class that may share events with the training draws and the
diagonal. For the boosted trees, the tree-structured
Parzen estimator of Optuna \cite{akiba_optuna_2019} runs 200 trials that maximize the
balanced accuracy of stratified five-fold cross-validation on that draw, over
boosting rounds in $[200, 2000]$, maximum depth in $[3, 12]$, learning rate
in $[0.01, 0.3]$, row and column subsampling in $[0.5, 1.0]$, minimum child
weight in $[1, 50]$, and $L_1$ and $L_2$ regularization in $[10^{-8}, 10]$.
The learning rate, the child weight and both regularization terms are
sampled log-uniformly. For the logistic regression, the same cross-validation
selects $C$ among 13 log-spaced values between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{3}$. The
configuration of a source stays fixed across its 20 draws and all its
targets.

Each target is scored by the recall of every class it holds and, when it
holds all the classes of the matrix, by their geometric mean,
\begin{equation}
G_m = \left( \prod_{c=1}^{C} \mathrm{recall}_c \right)^{1/C},
\end{equation}
which rewards recovering every class regardless of their proportion in the
target. Scores are computed on up to 5{,}000 events per class of the target,
drawn at random. Which descriptors each transferred model relies on is measured by
permutation importance, the drop in balanced accuracy averaged over three
permutations of one descriptor. It is computed on every source--target
pair, the diagonal included, over the first three draws and up to 1{,}000
events of each target class above the support threshold.

\subsection{Label budget}\label{subsec:budget}

The label budget measures how many labeled target events bring a transferred
model to the level of a model trained on the target. Each dataset with LP and
VT above the support threshold is a target in turn, and each of the other
nine is its source. In each of three repetitions, the target is split into a
fixed test set, half of each class up to 300 events, and a pool that keeps
the natural composition of the target. The source contributes 100 events per class. To these, $n$ labeled target
events are added from the pool, with $n$ in $\{0, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200\}$
in total rather than per class, or the whole pool where it is smaller. The
$n$ events are chosen in three ways. The random draw takes them at random.
The stratified draw takes half from each class, and the other class
completes the draw where one falls short. Active learning starts from the
source model and, in batches of 10, labels the pool events with the
smallest margin $|p(\mathrm{LP}) - 0.5|$ and retrains; this margin is a form
of the uncertainty sampling that \cite{bueno_continuous_2022} applied to
volcano-seismic monitoring, whereas \cite{manley_deep_2022} select by
diversity. Two references accompany the curve: the target-only model, the
same model trained on the $n$ target events alone, random or stratified,
and the local model, trained on up to 100 events per class of the pool.

Three classifiers enter the budget. The logistic regression and the boosted
trees keep the configuration tuned for each source in the transfer matrix
and use balanced class weights. The target-only and local models take the
configuration tuned for the target. Below 200 events, the target-only
boosted trees take a fixed configuration for small samples, 200 rounds of
depth 3, a learning rate of 0.1 and no minimum child weight or $L_1$ term,
since the tuned ones cannot split a node on so few events. TabPFN
\cite{hollmann_accurate_2025}, version 2, a transformer pretrained on
synthetic tabular tasks that classifies a test set in context from the
training set without gradient updates, enters with its default
configuration, without class weights, and an ensemble of four, with random
selection only. Every model is scored by $G_m$ on the fixed
test set, and the curve of each target is the median over its sources of the
mean over repetitions.

\subsection{Automatic segmentation}\label{subsec:autocut}

Segmentation by hand is part of the burden of adding a dataset, so the
validation events are also cut with the automatic proposal of
Section~\ref{subsec:manualcut} alone, its onset and end of the coda,
hereafter the automatic cut as against the manual cut. Their sixteen
descriptors are recomputed with the same code, and both cuts cover the same
LP and VT events. The comparison measures the shift of the onset and of the
end of the coda, the share of proposals the analyst accepted unchanged and
the rank correlation of $FI$, $DET$ and $SR$ between the cuts. The change
in $G_m$ is measured in two settings. Within each dataset that holds LP and
VT, the fixed configurations of the within-dataset classification are
scored by five-fold cross-validation over five draws of up to 100 events
per class, with the folds paired between the cuts. As a transfer target of
the five OVDAS sources, whose events keep their catalog onset, each dataset
is scored in $G_m$ or in the recall of its single class with the
configurations and the 20 draws of the transfer matrix.

\subsection{Checks of the transfer design}\label{subsec:checks}

Two checks, each over five training draws, probe the two choices of the
transfer design that a new dataset cannot verify without labels: when the
training events of the source were recorded, and in what proportion the
classes occur in the target. In the first, the 100 events per class of each source are drawn
systematically in time instead of at random: one every $N/100$ events of
the class in time order, from a random offset, with the same seed for the
model. A source catalog spanning years thus enters with its whole history.
In the second, each target is scored with all its LP and VT events, in its
natural composition, and the share of LP is estimated without its labels in
two ways. Black-box shift estimation \cite{lipton_detecting_2018} uses the
confusion matrix of the source model under five-fold cross-validation of
its training draw, which also fits a Platt calibration. The
expectation--maximization of \cite{saerens_adjusting_2002} runs on the
Platt-calibrated probabilities. The calibrated probabilities are then
reweighted by the estimated prior and the event is assigned to the most
probable class; the reweighting with the true prior bounds what a perfect
estimate would give. Each target is scored by its accuracy and, where it holds both classes,
by $G_m$.

\subsection{Audit of the expert labels}\label{subsec:audit}

Confident learning \cite{northcutt_confident_2021} flags the events whose
catalog label disagrees with the rest of the dataset. Within each dataset,
with every class of at least 50 events and at most 20{,}000 drawn at random
per class, two models give out-of-sample probabilities from five-fold
cross-validation on the sixteen descriptors, scaled with the median and
interquartile range of the dataset: the boosted trees with a fixed
configuration (300 rounds of depth 6, a learning rate of 0.1 and subsampling
of 0.8), the main model, and a logistic regression ($C = 1$) that checks
them. These probabilities estimate the joint distribution of given and true
labels, and the events whose label falls in an off-diagonal cell, pruned by
the estimated noise rate of each class, are flagged as suspect.

Unsupervised clustering tests whether the labeled classes form dense groups
in descriptor space. Within each dataset, HDBSCAN
\cite{campello_density-based_2013} and, as a reference, DBSCAN
\cite{ester_density-based_1996} group the
sixteen scaled descriptors reduced by principal components to 90\% of their
variance, on a random subsample of up to 4{,}000 events drawn regardless of
class. Their parameters are chosen with 80 trials of the tree-structured Parzen
estimator of Optuna that maximize the density-based clustering validation index (DBCV)
\cite{moulavi_density-based_2014}, so the labels take no part in the fit; the
agreement with the labels is measured afterwards by the adjusted Rand index,
and the DBCV of the expert labels themselves, in the same space, measures
whether the classes form dense, separated groups. The same procedure runs
on the amplitude spectrum of the window, 96 bins of 0.1~Hz between 0.5 and
10~Hz in log amplitude, centered per event and reduced by principal
components to 90\% of its variance, on the same events, to test whether
the absence of groups is a property of the sixteen descriptors or of the
signals (Appendix~\ref{subsec:supp_fft}).
In the four datasets that hold HB, the overlap of HB with LP and with VT is
also measured directly: the three classes are drawn as densities in the
plane of $SR$ and $FI$, and each pair of classes is separated by a logistic
regression on the sixteen descriptors, scored by balanced accuracy over
five-fold cross-validation on ten balanced draws of up to 500 events per
class.

Across datasets, the same trees, with balanced class weights, are trained
on up to 1{,}000 events per class of a source and predict up to 2{,}000
events per class of a target, on the events of the transfer matrices. The
classes are those among LP, VT and HB that both datasets hold above the
support threshold, for every pair with at least two shared classes, in
three repetitions. The share of target labels flagged with these
probabilities is compared with the share flagged with the out-of-sample
probabilities of the target itself on the same events. The per-class
thresholds of confident learning absorb a shift of all the probabilities,
so an excess of flags over the own criterion that appears in one class and
not in the others points to a difference in how that class is labeled.

To read what the flags mean, an analyst reviews at Hudson the 200 events
with the lowest label quality
among those flagged by both models, seeing the trace and the spectrogram with
the catalog class and the suggested class, and confirms or relabels each one.

Since the OVDAS analysts distinguish HB from LP by an impulsive,
high-frequency onset, the share of energy above 4.5~Hz in the first 2~s
after the onset, $HF_{\text{onset}}$, is measured on HB and LP events. At
Hudson and Copahue it is measured on up to 2{,}000 events of each class
drawn evenly across deciles of SNR, and at Nevado del Ruiz on every HB and
LP event of Olleta and Bis from the manual onset. It is compared between HB
and LP within each quartile of SNR. At Hudson, the share of HB among HB and
LP events is computed in each quintile of SNR.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

The results follow the order of the methods: transfer with no target labels,
the label budget, the automatic segmentation, the two checks and the audit of
the labels. Unless stated otherwise, a range of one score spans the two
classifiers.

\subsection{Transfer with no target labels}\label{subsec:res_transfer}

Within each dataset, with all its classes, the descriptors separate the
taxonomy to a degree that falls with the number and the overlap of its
classes (Figure~\ref{fig:confusion}, Appendix~\ref{subsec:supp_classif}). With the boosted trees, the balanced accuracy is 0.97 at Laguna del Maule
and 0.80 to 0.94 at the five datasets with two classes, the three VCSEIS
regions, Deception Island in 2009 and Veniaminof. It falls to 0.81 at
Villarrica with three classes and 0.75 at Nevados de Chill\'an with five, to
0.62 to 0.67 at Copahue, Hudson and both stations of Nevado del Ruiz, which
hold HB, to 0.53 in Alaska with four, and to 0.45 and 0.51 at Pavlof and
Semisopochnoi with six, against chance levels of 0.17 to 0.50.

Six descriptors keep the direction of their contrast between LP and VT at
all ten datasets that hold both classes: $FI$, $DET$, $SR$,
$f_{\text{peak}}$, $W$ and $W_{entr}$ (Table~\ref{tab:transferability} and
Figure~\ref{fig:aucsystems}, Appendix~\ref{subsec:res_transferability}). $FI$ leads the ranking with a median oriented AUC of
0.93, lowest in Alaska (0.73), followed by $DET$ and $SR$ (0.87); the other

\subsection{Automatic segmentation}\label{subsec:res_autocut}

Where the catalog gives a reference, the automatic cut leaves the descriptors
and the scores unchanged. At the four VCSEIS regions and the two Deception Island deployments, the
analyst accepted 37\% to 81\% of the proposals unchanged. The onset moves by
more than 1~s in 2\% to 5\% of the events, except at Deception Island in
2009 (35\%), and the rank correlation of $FI$, $DET$ and $SR$ between the
cuts is 0.96 to 1.00. Within each VCSEIS region the $G_m$ changes by at most
0.01, and as a transfer target by less than 0.01. At Nevado del Ruiz, whose
files carry no reference, the analyst accepted 0\% to 4\% of the proposals,
the correlation falls to 0.78 to 0.93, and the $G_m$ within Olleta drops by
0.01 to 0.02.

The VOISS-Net windows, labeled spans of 120~s with no arrival, are where the

more than 1~s in 40\% to 57\% of the events, and the correlation of the

\subsection{Checks of the transfer design}\label{subsec:res_checks}

Drawing the training events of each source evenly in time leaves the transfer
unchanged: the median change in $G_m$ per target is within 0.01 for both
classifiers, and 4 of the 180 combinations of pair and classifier change by
more than 0.05, in either direction.

Correcting the prior of the target, estimated without its labels, helps
only the targets with a single class. At the six VOISS-Net targets the
accuracy rises, at Redoubt from 0.61 to 0.63 to 0.76 to 0.81, at
K\={\i}lauea from 0.72 to 0.74 to 0.80 to 0.86 and at Tanaga from 0.87 to
0.89 to 0.99 to 1.00, while at Nevado del Ruiz at Bis, where the transfer
fails, it falls from 0.05 to 0.07 to 0.02.

Wherever both classes are present, the correction lowers $G_m$. The estimate of the LP share misses by a median of 0.01 to 0.13 at the
other targets with both classes and by 0.36 to 0.45 at Hudson and Nevado
del Ruiz at Olleta. The reweighting lowers $G_m$ by up to 0.05 where the
prior is near balance, from 0.78 to 0.84 to 0.71 to 0.75 at Copahue, from
0.85 to 0.87 to 0.45 to 0.46 at Laguna del Maule, to 0 at Villarrica, and
from 0.66 to 0.67 to 0.35 to 0.42 at Hudson.
Reweighting with the true prior lowers $G_m$ as much at Laguna del Maule (to
0.62 to 0.63) and Villarrica (to 0 to 0.06), so there the loss follows from
the reweighting itself, while at Hudson it raises $G_m$ to 0.70 to 0.72 and
the loss follows from the error of the estimate.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The number of expert labels a new dataset needs is set by where it starts.
With no target labels, the ten targets span a $G_m$ of 0.45 to 0.89, and
that score, which costs no labels to measure, predicts what each label buys:
at Nevados de Chill\'an or the Cascade Range a hundred labels add 0.01 to
0.02, at Hudson they add 0.17, and at Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta the whole
pool of 157 is not enough. The budget is therefore a function of the initial
gap rather than a fixed number, and the first step at a new dataset is to
measure the transfer from the available sources before labeling anything.
Where that transfer starts within a few hundredths of the local model, as at
Nevados de Chill\'an and the Cascade Range, the transferred model serves as
it is or with 20 to 50 labels. Elsewhere, about 100 target labels drawn from
both classes bring a model within 0.03 of the local level at nine of the ten
targets; the exception, Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta, is the target whose LP
criterion differs from that of the sources.

What transfers is the pair of global classes, carried by a small set of
descriptors. $FI$, $DET$ and $SR$ keep their direction in all ten datasets and dominate
the transferred models, and in the plane of $FI$ and $DET$, LP and VT occupy
the same regions at every catalog but Nevado del Ruiz, whose LP lie in the
core of VT. The loss of 0.07 to 0.09 is the same when the volcano
changes within one agency and when the agency changes, so for LP and VT the
labeling agency is not a barrier. HB behaves as a different class in each
dataset, between LP and VT at Copahue, at the edge of LP at Hudson and
within VT at Nevado del Ruiz, and its recall falls from 0.57 to 0.61 within
a dataset to 0.15 to 0.20 between datasets. The taxonomy that transfers thus
has two levels: global classes that a classifier carries across datasets,
and local classes that each catalog defines for itself. A dataset can also fall
outside the transfer altogether: the LP of Nevado del Ruiz at Bis, events
the SGC records there because they are not visible at Olleta, are recovered
with a median recall of 0.03 to 0.05 over all sources.

The bottleneck is the definition of the class, not the classifier. The
criterion of another dataset flags LP and VT labels at about the dataset's
own rate at most targets, and 40\% to 70\% of the HB labels against 10\% to
36\% with the own criterion;
the disagreement holds between Hudson and Copahue, two catalogs of one
observatory, where each sees the HB of the other as its own VT or LP. At the thirteen datasets with more than 200 events, no unsupervised grouping
reproduces the classes, on the sixteen descriptors or on the full amplitude
spectrum, and the expert labels have a negative DBCV in all nine audited
datasets. The classes are therefore operational cuts in a
continuum of events, a continuum that does not depend on the
representation, and a classifier learns the cut of the catalog it was
trained on. The two Deception Island deployments, where the spectrum does
recover LP and VT, are the smallest datasets, and the 2009 deployment is the
cleanest in the audit;
they show what a taxonomy of well separated classes looks like when it
exists. Unifying criteria before transfer is thus not a matter of agreeing
on names but of agreeing on the observable feature that defines each cut.
The audit has a limit of its own. Confident learning assumes a noise that
depends only on the true class, while the HB noise depends on a feature of
the event, so the method locates the disagreement without measuring it free
of bias. The manual review at Hudson saw the suggested class and followed
it in 120 of 141 changes, so it measures agreement with the model as much
as the quality of the labels.

What the HB label measures at OVDAS is the source together with the
recording. The analysts distinguish HB from LP by a high-frequency onset, and the
label follows that feature consistently: within each SNR quartile the
$HF_{\text{onset}}$ of HB is 2 to 4.5 times that of LP at Hudson and
Copahue, and the HB that the review returned to LP are those whose onset
lacks it. The share of HB among HB and LP events at Hudson rises from 1\% to 14\%
from the lowest to the highest quintile of SNR, as expected if the feature
depends on the energy of the event and on the attenuation along the path.
The same source process can then be labeled LP when the event is small or
distant and HB when it is large or close, and the label carries the event
size, the source--station distance and the site along with the source. This
is consistent with the failure of HB to transfer even between two volcanoes
of one observatory, and at Nevado del Ruiz, where the onset feature does not
separate HB from LP, the SGC analysts label HB by another criterion. A class defined this way needs an operational
definition that names the feature and corrects it for the recording, for
instance a threshold on $HF_{\text{onset}}$ conditioned on SNR, or a status
as a subtype of LP with an impulsive onset. LP, VT and AV, whose
definitions are closer to a standard, do not raise this problem to the same
degree.

The plane of Figure~\ref{fig:plane} suggests how such definitions can be
put to work: a classification in two stages. The first stage separates LP
from VT with the transferable descriptors, and it is the stage that a new
dataset inherits from another with no labels or with a few, since the two clouds keep their places in every catalog
but Nevado del Ruiz. The second stage acts within
each cloud and assigns the local classes with one operational feature each.
On the LP side, HB is told by $HF_{\text{onset}}$ conditioned on SNR,
TO by the sharpness of its spectral peak and the length of its
quasi-monochromatic coda, and VLP by the energy below 1~Hz, which the
present band leaves out. Within the VT cloud, where AV, the regional and
other events of Alaska and the tremor and HB of Nevado del Ruiz fall, AV is
told by its emergent onset and duration and the regional events by their
distance. The classes that lie between the two clouds, VLP, TO, the HB of
Copahue and the AV of Hudson, are those the first stage divides, and the
second stage receives them with their own feature rather than with the
sixteen descriptors. The cascade turns the two-level taxonomy into a
procedure: a global stage that transfers, and a local stage that each
observatory defines on features it can name and measure. Its test, the
balanced accuracy of the cascade against that of the flat classification
of Section~\ref{subsec:res_transfer}, 0.75 at Nevados de Chill\'an and
0.62 to 0.67 at Copahue and Hudson, is left for a later study.

The methods that need no target labels do not close the gap. Drawing the
training events across the history of the source changes nothing, so the
transfer loss is not a matter of when the source events were recorded.
Correcting the class prior of the target raises accuracy only at the
targets that hold one class and that the model already recovers, and lowers
$G_m$ wherever the target holds two. The
correction maximizes accuracy, and with a prior far from balance that
sacrifices the minority class, which is the class $G_m$ protects, as the
reweighting with the true prior at Laguna del Maule and Villarrica shows.
Where the transferred model is weak, as at Hudson, the estimate of
the prior inherits its errors. These corrections suit a target dominated by
one class and a model that already transfers, not a target where the labels
are needed.

Among the methods that use target labels, the gain comes from the model more
than from the choice of events. TabPFN reaches 0.88 at Hudson with 20 random labels, within 0.02 of the
logistic regression with 100, and trained on 10 target events alone it
comes within 0.03 of the local model. At Nevado del Ruiz at Olleta the
target-only model outperforms every model that keeps the source, so a
source far from the target is better discarded. Active
learning by uncertainty adds 0.01 to 0.03, as expected when the LP--VT
boundary is already well placed by the source, in line with the gain that \cite{manley_deep_2022} found for less separable
data and not for well separated ones. Class imbalance defeats random and
uncertainty-based selection together, because neither sees the rare class,
and a stratified draw repairs it completely: at Villarrica 100 stratified
target labels reach the local model where 200 random ones leave it at zero.
A stratified draw, however, presumes that the analyst can find the rare
class before labeling, that is, a criterion or a detector for it, which is
the assumption the budget has to state.

Segmentation by hand pays only where the catalog gives no reference. With a
catalog arrival, the automatic cut leaves the descriptors and the scores
unchanged; without one, at Nevado del Ruiz, it costs 0.01 to 0.02 of $G_m$,
and in the 120~s windows of VOISS-Net it moves the recall of VT by up to
0.17 in either direction. The cost of adding a dataset therefore lies in the
labels, not in the onsets, provided the catalog publishes a time of arrival
with each label; a catalog of labeled windows without arrivals makes the
10~s descriptors depend on the detector.

Together, the results give a protocol for a new dataset: measure the
transfer from the available sources with no labels; where it falls short,
label about 100 events, half from each class, and consider a model trained
on the target alone; before any local class, fix its operational definition
on a measurable feature corrected for the recording, starting with HB; audit
the labels with confident learning within the dataset and against a
reference source, and review the suspects; and automate the segmentation
wherever the catalog supplies arrivals.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{subsec:limitations}

The OVDAS datasets use one station per volcano and its vertical channel, a
choice of this study, although each local network holds several stations.
With one station, the recording conditions of a dataset cannot be separated
from its labels, and they enter the label of HB and possibly of AV; a
network approach, which classifies each event from several stations as
multi-station classifiers do \cite{salazar_multi-station_2023,ferreira_deep_2023},
is the natural extension. The conclusions also rest on descriptors of a
10~s window, within which
VLP and tremor lie partly outside the band; the coda-length window leaves
the LP--VT transfer unchanged but was not carried into the budget. The
budget and the audit across datasets cover the two global classes, so the
number of labels is measured on LP and VT and not on a full taxonomy, whose
within-dataset accuracy is 0.45 to 0.81, and HB enters the audit only in
the four datasets that label it. One analyst segmented the validation records and
reviewed the suspects at Hudson, and the review saw the suggested class; a
blind review by several analysts, as in \cite{anzieta_cleaning_2023}, would
separate the quality of the labels from the agreement with the model.
Confident learning ran with models of fixed configuration. The budget and the cross-dataset audit use three
repetitions, the checks five and the matrices twenty. TabPFN enters with an
ensemble of four and without active learning. The validation records at 50
and 40~Hz are resampled, and Olleta and Bis hold few events of each class.
The evidence on the HB criterion comes from two OVDAS datasets and one SGC
volcano, so the dependence on SNR is established at OVDAS and the
alternative criterion at the SGC remains to be named.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

How many expert labels a new dataset needs is set by how far a classifier
trained elsewhere already carries it, and that distance can be measured
before any label is drawn. Across twenty datasets from five catalogs, LP
and VT transfer between volcanoes and between labeling agencies with the
same loss, carried by three descriptors, and about 100 target labels, drawn
half from each class, bring a transferred model to the level of a local one
at eight of ten targets. Where the transfer starts low, a model pretrained
on tabular tasks reaches with 20 labels what a tuned classifier reaches
with 100, and a model trained on the target labels alone outperforms any
that keeps a distant source; active learning adds little, and the
corrections that need no labels close no gap. The remaining distance is in
the labels: the global classes keep one criterion across agencies, while
HB is a different class in each catalog, including two of one observatory,
because the feature its analysts use, a high-frequency onset, depends on
the energy of the event and on the recording. Adding a dataset to an
automatic classifier therefore begins with a definition of each local class
on a feature that can be measured and corrected for the recording, follows
with a stratified budget of about 100 labels per class and an audit of
those labels, and leaves the segmentation to the catalog wherever it
supplies arrivals.

\subsection{Coda-length window}\label{subsec:codawindow}

Many events outlast the 10~s window (Table~\ref{tab:durations}), which then
measures their onset and early coda. A second window follows each event to
the end of its coda, from 5~s, the shortest record that supports the
recurrence descriptors, to 100~s, after which a long coda is measured on its
first 100~s, as in the full-duration window of \cite{morales_recurrence_2026}.
The duration is the analyst-assigned one at the OVDAS datasets, where a
non-positive duration appears in at most 0.24\% of the analyzed events of a
dataset, and the end of the coda set by hand at the validation datasets. The window is cut from a
segment padded from 10~s before the onset to 3~s after its end and
conditioned as the fixed-length window, with a 5~s noise window from 7 to 2~s
before the onset (Figure~\ref{fig:codawindow}). At Laguna del Maule, Nevados
de Chill\'an, Copahue and Villarrica it is computed on a subsample of the
events lasting 5~s or more: every class above the support threshold enters
in full up to 10 times the size of the smallest class, or 100 times at
Villarrica, and a larger class contributes that many events taken at even
steps in time; Hudson enters in full. The descriptors and the transfer design
are those of the main text.

With this window, the diagonal of the LP--VT matrix reaches a median $G_m$ of
0.90 to 0.91 and the pairs outside it 0.78 to 0.83, and the two classifiers
differ by at most 0.05 at every step. $FI$ leads the ranking of
Table~\ref{tab:transferability} with a median AUC of 0.94, $SR$ rises to
second place (0.89) and $DET$ falls to 0.79 and reverses at Northern
California.

\subsection{Clustering on the amplitude spectrum}\label{subsec:supp_fft}

Figure~\ref{fig:fft} compares HDBSCAN on the amplitude spectrum of the
window with HDBSCAN on the sixteen descriptors, on the same events, at the
fifteen datasets with at least two classes of 20 events. The spectrum is the
FFT of the z-scored 10~s window with a Hann taper, 96 bins of 0.1~Hz between
0.5 and 10~Hz, in log amplitude and centered per event; both
representations are reduced by principal components to 90\% of their
variance, which keeps 40 to 68 components of the spectrum and 4 to 6 of the
descriptors, and the parameters of HDBSCAN are chosen by DBCV without the
labels, as in Section~\ref{subsec:audit}. A logistic regression on the raw
representation, scored by balanced accuracy over five-fold cross-validation
on up to 300 events per class, serves as a supervised control.
